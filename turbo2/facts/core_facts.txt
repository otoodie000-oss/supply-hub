# --- space
The Sun is a star at the center of our solar system.
The Sun is about 150 million kilometres from Earth.
Sunlight takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth.
There are eight planets in our solar system.
In order from the Sun, the planets are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, then Jupiter.
Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest planet to the Sun.
Venus is the hottest planet in our solar system because its thick clouds trap heat.
Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only planet known to have life.
Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and is called the Red Planet because of rusty iron in its soil.
Mars has two small moons called Phobos and Deimos.
Olympus Mons on Mars is the tallest volcano in the solar system.
Jupiter is the largest planet in our solar system, and it is a gas giant.
Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a giant storm bigger than Earth.
Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and is famous for its bright rings made of ice and rock.
Uranus is an ice giant that spins on its side.
Neptune is the farthest planet from the Sun and has the strongest winds in the solar system.
Pluto was called a planet until 2006, when it became a dwarf planet.
The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite, and it is about 384,000 kilometres away.
The Moon takes about 27 days to orbit Earth, and we always see the same side of it.
Neil Armstrong was the first person to walk on the Moon, in 1969.
Buzz Aldrin walked on the Moon with Neil Armstrong on Apollo 11.
Yuri Gagarin was the first human in space, in 1961.
A light-year is the distance light travels in one year.
A black hole is a place in space where gravity is so strong that not even light can escape.
The Milky Way is the galaxy that contains our solar system.
A galaxy is a huge group of stars, gas and dust held together by gravity.
The universe is about 13.8 billion years old.
A comet is a ball of ice and dust that grows a tail near the Sun.
An asteroid is a rocky object that orbits the Sun.
Most asteroids are in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.
A shooting star is really a meteor, a small piece of space rock burning up in Earth's air.
The International Space Station is a science lab that orbits Earth about every 90 minutes.
Stars twinkle because their light bends as it passes through Earth's moving air.
A solar eclipse happens when the Moon passes between Earth and the Sun.
A lunar eclipse happens when Earth's shadow falls on the Moon.
Earth takes about 365 and a quarter days to orbit the Sun.
Earth spins once on its axis about every 24 hours, which gives us day and night.
Seasons happen because Earth's axis is tilted about 23.5 degrees.
