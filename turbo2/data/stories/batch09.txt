## The Monkey and the Mirror | a monkey seeing its reflection
A curious young monkey named Ziggy found a mirror that someone had dropped in the jungle. He looked into it and saw another monkey staring back. Ziggy waved. The other monkey waved too. Ziggy made a funny face, and so did the other monkey. "Copycat!" Ziggy shouted, and he stuck out his tongue. The other monkey did the same. Ziggy got angry and pushed the mirror, but the other monkey pushed back at exactly the same time. Ziggy was confused and upset. His grandmother swung down from a branch and laughed. "That's you, little one. The mirror shows you yourself." Ziggy looked again, slowly this time. He smiled, and the monkey in the mirror smiled too. "So when I was grumpy, I was being grumpy at myself?" he asked. His grandmother nodded. "The world often shows you back what you bring to it," she said. From then on, Ziggy always greeted the mirror, and everyone else, with a big smile.

## The Marathon Grandpa | an old man running a marathon
When Grandpa Lek turned seventy, he announced that he was going to run a marathon. His family thought he was joking. A marathon is forty-two kilometers long! But Grandpa was serious. He started by walking around the park every morning. Then he jogged slowly between two trees. Then he jogged the whole park. His granddaughter, Pim, joined him on her bicycle, carrying water and cheering. After a year of training, the big day came. Thousands of runners lined up at the start. Grandpa was one of the oldest. He ran slowly and steadily, stopping to drink water and stretch. Many young runners passed him. Hours went by. Near the end, his legs ached and he wanted to stop. Then he heard Pim shouting from the crowd, "Go, Grandpa, go!" He kept going and crossed the finish line after six hours, one of the last runners. The crowd gave him the biggest cheer of the day. Pim hung the medal around his neck, and he wore it to dinner every night for a month.

## The Turtle Shell Drum | making music from found objects
In a small village, the children wanted to start a band, but nobody had any instruments. They looked around and got creative. Pong found an empty paint can and turned it into a drum. Nok filled bottles with different amounts of water and tapped them with a spoon to play different notes. Lek stretched rubber bands over a shoebox to make a little guitar. Fah put dried beans in a plastic bottle to make a shaker. They practiced every afternoon under the big tree in the village square. At first, it sounded terrible. But they kept trying, and slowly, their sounds began to fit together. The village elders came to listen. An old man who used to play in a band taught them some rhythms. At the village festival, the children performed with their homemade instruments. The crowd danced and clapped. A music teacher from the city heard about them and donated real instruments. But the children kept their paint can drum, because that's where their music began.

## The Lazy Cloud Day | a relaxing day doing nothing
Sometimes, the best days are the ones where nothing happens. That's what Mina learned on a hot Sunday when her whole family was too tired to go anywhere. At first, she was bored. Then her dad spread a blanket on the grass under the big tree. They lay down and looked up at the leaves and the sky. They watched an ant carry a crumb across the blanket. They heard a bird singing a long, complicated song. They saw a cloud that looked like a rabbit slowly turn into a teapot. Mom brought out cold watermelon slices, and they ate them with juice dripping down their chins. Mina's little brother fell asleep with his mouth open, and a butterfly landed on his nose. They all tried very hard not to laugh. By evening, Mina felt calm and happy in a way she couldn't explain. "We didn't do anything today," she said. Her dad smiled. "We did the most important thing," he said. "We noticed."

## The Puppy Who Wanted a Job | a puppy looking for work
On a busy farm, every animal had a job. The rooster woke everyone up. The cow gave milk. The sheepdog herded the sheep. The cat caught mice. But Buttons, the new puppy, didn't have a job, and he felt useless. He tried to herd the sheep, but they chased him instead. He tried to catch a mouse, but it ran between his legs. He tried to crow like the rooster, but only managed a squeaky yip. Buttons sat down by the farmhouse door, feeling sad. Just then, the farmer's little daughter, who was sick in bed with a fever, called out, "Is that a puppy? Can he come in?" Buttons trotted inside and jumped onto her bed. She giggled and hugged him. He licked her cheek and curled up beside her. The farmer saw his daughter smiling for the first time in days. "Well," he said, "it looks like Buttons has the most important job on the farm: making people feel better."

## The Riddle of the Sphinx Cat | a cat who asks riddles
In the back of an old bookshop lived a mysterious cat named Sphinx. Whenever a customer wanted a rare book, Sphinx sat on top of the shelf and blocked the way. To get past, you had to answer a riddle. "What has keys but can't open locks?" she would meow. Most customers didn't understand cat language, so they just waited until she moved. But a girl named Ruby could understand her. "A piano!" Ruby answered, and Sphinx purred and stepped aside. The next day, Sphinx asked, "What gets wetter the more it dries?" "A towel!" said Ruby. Every day, Ruby came to answer a new riddle and choose a new book. The bookshop owner noticed that Ruby was reading more than anyone in town. One day, Ruby brought her own riddle. "What can you hold in your right hand but never in your left?" Sphinx thought and thought, her tail twitching. Finally, Ruby whispered the answer: "Your left elbow!" Sphinx was so impressed that she let Ruby pet her, which she had never let anyone do before.

## The Fisherman's Net | sharing and generosity
In a small fishing village, a fisherman named Udom caught more fish than anyone else. His net was strong and wide, and he knew the best places to fish. But Udom never shared. He sold all his fish at high prices, even to families who were struggling. One season, a storm destroyed Udom's boat and net. He had nothing left. He went from door to door asking for help, expecting everyone to turn him away, because he had never helped them. But the villagers remembered something else. Udom had once taught many of them how to fix nets and read the weather. So they helped him. One family gave him old wood for a new boat. Another gave him rope. The children collected scraps to weave a new net. When Udom sailed out again, he caught a huge load of fish. This time, he shared it with every family in the village. From then on, Udom was known not as the best fisherman, but as the most generous one.

## The Raindrop Race | raindrops racing down a window
On a rainy afternoon, Kaito sat by the window, watching raindrops slide down the glass. He picked two drops and named them. The fat one on the left was Bolt. The small one on the right was Zippy. "Ready, set, go!" he whispered. Bolt started fast, gobbling up smaller drops and getting bigger and faster. Zippy moved slowly, wiggling side to side. Kaito's little sister, Hana, joined him and cheered for Zippy. Bolt was winning by a lot, but then it hit a dry patch on the glass and stopped completely. Zippy kept wiggling along until it bumped into another drop, joined it and suddenly zoomed down to the bottom. Zippy won! Hana jumped up and down. They raced raindrops for an hour, naming each one and cheering loudly. Their mom came in and asked what all the noise was about. "The Rain Olympics," said Kaito. Mom laughed and sat down to watch. That rainy afternoon turned out to be the most fun day of the whole week.

## The Spider's Masterpiece | a spider building a web
Every night, a spider named Ada spun a beautiful web in the corner of the garden. It was round and perfect, with silky threads that sparkled with dew in the morning. Every morning, the gardener swept it away with his broom. Ada was frustrated, but she built it again the next night. Night after night, the same thing happened. One morning, the gardener's grandson, Theo, saw the web before his grandfather could sweep it. The sun was rising behind it, and the dew drops shone like tiny diamonds. "Grandpa, wait! Look!" The gardener stopped and looked closely. He had never really noticed the web before. It was beautiful. Theo took a photo of it and entered it into a school photography contest. He won first prize. After that, the gardener left a small corner of the garden just for Ada. She spun her webs there every night, and every morning, Theo came out to see her newest masterpiece glittering in the sunlight.

## The Boy Who Shared His Umbrella | sharing an umbrella in the rain
On a stormy afternoon, Nat walked home from school with his big blue umbrella. At the bus stop, he saw an old woman standing in the rain with no umbrella, holding heavy grocery bags. Nat walked over and held his umbrella above her head. "Where are you going?" he asked. She pointed down the street, the opposite direction from his home. Nat walked with her anyway, holding the umbrella and helping carry one of her bags. They talked the whole way. She told him she used to be a teacher and taught math for forty years. When they reached her house, she invited him in for hot cocoa and gave him a book of math puzzles. Nat got home late and soaking wet, and his mom was worried. But when he explained, she hugged him. The next week, Nat visited the old woman again, and she helped him with his math homework. They became good friends. Nat learned that sharing an umbrella can lead to a lot more than staying dry.

## The Firefly in the Jar | letting something go free
On a warm summer night, a boy named Sam caught a firefly in a glass jar. He watched it blink its soft yellow light and decided to keep it forever as a night light. He put the jar by his bed, with holes in the lid and a little leaf inside. The first night, the firefly glowed brightly. The second night, its light was dimmer. On the third night, it barely glowed at all, sitting still on the bottom of the jar. Sam felt worried. He asked his grandmother what was wrong. "Fireflies need to fly free, to find their friends and dance in the dark," she said gently. "A jar is too small for a life like that." Sam felt sad, but he understood. He carried the jar outside, opened the lid and waited. The firefly crawled to the edge, spread its wings and flew up into the night, glowing brighter than ever. Soon it joined hundreds of others in the field. Sam smiled. It was more beautiful free than it had ever been in his jar.

## The Two Snowmen | two snowmen becoming friends
After a big snowfall, two families on the same street each built a snowman in their front yards. One snowman, built by the Smith twins, was tall and proud, with a top hat and a carrot nose. The other, built by little Amy, was short and lumpy, with a scarf that was too long and pebbles for eyes. The tall snowman looked down at the little one. "You're not a very good snowman," he said. The little snowman just smiled with her pebble mouth. That night, a strong wind blew. The tall snowman's top hat flew off and his head wobbled. "Help!" he cried. The little snowman couldn't move, but she called to a passing cat, who knocked the hat back near him and leaned against his side until the wind stopped. In the morning, the tall snowman said, "Thank you. I was wrong about you." When the sun came out, both snowmen began to melt, but they melted together, side by side, as friends.

## The Bookworm's Journey | a worm traveling through books
Inside an old library lived a tiny bookworm named Barnaby who ate his way through books. But Barnaby didn't just eat. He read every page as he went. He crawled through a book about pirates and learned how to tie sailor's knots. He chewed through a cookbook and learned how to make pancakes, though he had no kitchen. He tunneled through an atlas and visited every country in the world, page by page. One day, Barnaby reached the very last book on the very last shelf. It was a book with blank pages. Barnaby was confused. Then he had an idea. With his tiny body, he began to wiggle across the pages, leaving little trails that looked like letters. He wrote about all the books he had read and all the places he had been. When the librarian found the book, she couldn't believe her eyes. Somebody had written a whole story inside it in the tiniest handwriting she had ever seen. She put it on the front shelf, where it is still read today.

## The Night Sky Map | learning constellations
Every clear night, Grandpa took Lina onto the roof to look at the stars. He had an old paper map of the constellations, and he taught her how to find them. "First, find the brightest stars," he said. "Then connect them like dots." Lina found three stars in a row. "That's Orion's belt," said Grandpa. "He's a hunter in the old stories." She found a group of seven tiny stars close together. "Those are the Pleiades, the seven sisters." Lina loved learning the stories behind each shape. But she also started making up her own. She saw a cat chasing a fish, a teapot and a dancing elephant. Grandpa laughed. "The ancient people did the same thing," he said. "They looked up and saw their own stories." Years later, Lina became a scientist who studies stars with giant telescopes. But on clear nights, she still climbs onto her roof with her old map and looks for her dancing elephant.
