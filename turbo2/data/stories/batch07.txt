## The Giraffe Who Was Too Tall | a giraffe who felt too tall
Gina the giraffe was the tallest animal on the savanna, and she hated it. She bumped her head on branches and couldn't fit in the shade where the zebras rested. When the animals played hide and seek, she was always found first. One day, a little meerkat went missing. The animals searched everywhere but couldn't find him in the tall grass. Gina stretched her long neck as high as it could go and looked out across the whole savanna. Far away, she spotted the meerkat stuck on a rock in the middle of a stream. She ran over and gently lifted him out with her mouth. The meerkat's family hugged her legs. That evening, the animals asked Gina to be their lookout. She could see storms coming, lions approaching and lost friends from far away. Gina realized that being different wasn't a problem. It was her special way of helping everyone, and she stood a little taller with pride.

## The Lost Words | a boy who lost his words
One morning, Rafi woke up and found that he had lost all his words. He opened his mouth to say good morning, but nothing came out. He tried to write, but the pencil just made squiggles. At breakfast, he pointed at the milk and his mom understood. At school, his friend Ana noticed something was wrong. Instead of laughing, she started drawing pictures to ask him questions. Rafi drew back. They played tic-tac-toe, built a tower of blocks and laughed together without saying a single word. At recess, they played tag. Rafi realized that friendship didn't need many words. A smile, a wave or a shared snack said a lot. By evening, his words started coming back, one by one: "Mom," then "milk," then "thank you." The first full sentence he said was to Ana the next morning: "Thank you for understanding me without words." Ana smiled and drew him a heart.

## The Banana Thief | monkeys stealing bananas
At a fruit stand near the temple, a mischievous monkey named Bobo kept stealing bananas. Every afternoon, he swung down from the trees, grabbed a bunch and raced away before the fruit seller, Mrs. Sri, could catch him. She tried everything. She covered the bananas with a cloth. She hung up shiny tin cans to scare him. She even bought a rubber snake. Bobo just laughed and kept stealing. One day, Mrs. Sri sat down and watched Bobo carefully. She noticed that he didn't eat the bananas himself. He carried them up into the trees, where his old grandmother monkey, too weak to find food, was waiting. Mrs. Sri's heart softened. The next day, she left a small bunch of bananas on a branch near her stand with a little sign that said "For Bobo." Bobo took them and, from then on, never stole again. Sometimes he even scared away birds from her fruit, as if to say thank you.

## The Clockmaker's Apprentice | learning a careful craft
Lucas wanted to become a clockmaker, so he became the apprentice of old Mr. Frost, who made the finest clocks in town. On his first day, Lucas was excited to build a clock. But Mr. Frost handed him a broom. "First, sweep the workshop." On the second day, Lucas organized tiny screws by size. On the third day, he cleaned gears with a soft brush. This went on for weeks. Lucas grew frustrated. "When will I build a clock?" Mr. Frost smiled and gave him a broken pocket watch. "Fix this." Lucas opened it and was amazed. He knew every gear and screw. He knew which ones were dirty and which were bent, because he had cleaned and sorted thousands of them. He fixed the watch in one afternoon, and it ticked perfectly. "A clock," said Mr. Frost, "is made of a thousand small things done right. You've been learning to build clocks this whole time."

## The Shadow That Ran Away | a boy chasing his shadow
On a bright sunny morning, Pip's shadow decided it was tired of following him around. It peeled itself off the ground and ran away down the street. Pip chased after it. The shadow hopped over fences, danced with a lamp post's shadow and hid behind a tree. Pip couldn't catch it. He sat down, out of breath. The shadow peeked out from behind the tree. "Why do you want to leave?" Pip asked. "You always walk where you want," said the shadow. "I never get to choose." Pip thought about it. "How about we take turns? In the morning, I lead. In the afternoon, you lead." The shadow agreed. That afternoon, Pip followed his shadow wherever it went. It led him to the park, where the shadows of the trees made a beautiful lace pattern on the grass, and to the river, where shadows of fish swam among the rocks. Pip had never noticed shadows were so beautiful. From then on, they were the best of friends.

## The Girl Who Fixed Things | a young repair genius
Everybody in the neighborhood brought their broken things to ten-year-old Mai. She fixed a toaster with a loose wire, a bicycle with a slipping chain and a radio that only played static. Mai didn't know everything, but she was patient. When something was broken, she studied it, took it apart carefully, laid out every piece in order and figured out how it worked. One day, the old man next door brought her a music box that had belonged to his late wife. It hadn't played in twenty years. Mai worked on it for a whole week. She cleaned the tiny comb, straightened a bent pin and oiled the spring. Finally, she wound it up, and a gentle waltz began to play. The old man closed his eyes and smiled. "That was the song we danced to at our wedding," he said softly. Mai realized that sometimes when you fix something, you're also fixing a memory.

## The Lonely Planet | a small planet looking for friends
Far out in space, a small planet named Tiko floated all alone. It had no moons, no rings and no nearby neighbors. It watched the big planets in other solar systems with their moons and comets and felt lonely. One day, a wandering comet passed nearby, its icy tail glowing. "Why are you sad?" asked the comet. "I have no friends," said Tiko. The comet laughed. "Look closer." Tiko looked at its own surface. There were mountains and valleys, and tiny clouds swirling above them. And in one valley, a small green plant had begun to grow. Then another. Life was starting on Tiko! "You're never alone," said the comet, "when you're a home for something." Over millions of years, Tiko grew forests, rivers and creatures of every kind. It became the busiest, liveliest planet in the galaxy. And every few hundred years, the old comet passed by again and waved its glowing tail hello.

## The Library Cat | a cat who lives in a library
Dewey was a fluffy orange cat who lived in the town library. He slept on top of the warm computer, napped in the poetry section and greeted every visitor at the door. Children loved reading to him. A shy boy named Sam, who struggled with reading, came every Thursday. He was too embarrassed to read to the librarian, but he read to Dewey. Dewey never laughed when Sam stumbled on a word. He just purred and blinked his slow, happy eyes. Week by week, Sam read a little faster and a little more smoothly. By the end of the year, he finished a whole chapter book. The librarian gave Sam a certificate, and Dewey got a fancy new cushion. Sam grew up to be a teacher. Years later, he started a reading program at his school where shy kids read to animals. He called it the Dewey Program, after the patient orange cat who had believed in him first.

## The River That Forgot Its Way | a river finding the sea
High in the mountains, a young river was born from melting snow. It tumbled down the rocks, excited to reach the sea it had heard so much about. But halfway down, it reached a desert. The sand drank up its water, and the river began to disappear. "I'll never reach the sea," it cried. The wind heard and said, "Let me carry you." The river was afraid. "If I turn into vapor and rise into the sky, I won't be a river anymore." "You'll still be water," said the wind. "And water always finds its way." So the river let itself rise as mist into the air. The wind carried it across the desert as clouds. On the other side, the clouds rained down on the mountains, and the water gathered into a new river that flowed all the way to the sea. When it finally reached the waves, the river laughed. It had changed its shape, but never lost itself.

## The Taco Truck Race | a funny food truck race
In a busy city, two food trucks parked on the same corner every day: Taco Tony and Burger Bella. They were rivals. They tried to outdo each other with louder music, brighter signs and bigger portions. One day, the city announced a food truck race to the beach festival. The winner would get the best spot. Tony and Bella raced through traffic, honking and waving. But on the highway, Tony's truck got a flat tire. Bella zoomed past, smiling. Then she looked in her mirror and saw Tony standing by the road, looking defeated. She sighed and pulled over. "Need a hand?" They changed the tire together, covered in grease and laughing. They arrived at the festival last. But the festival organizers had seen everything on a news camera. They gave them a shared spot right in the middle, under a sign that said "Best Teamwork." Tony and Bella started selling a new combo: the Burger Taco. It was surprisingly delicious.

## The Owl Who Couldn't Hoot | an owl with a different voice
Little Ollie the owl couldn't hoot. When he tried, he made a funny chirping sound, like a bird singing. The other owls teased him. "Owls hoot! What kind of owl chirps?" Ollie flew away and sat sadly on a branch. A nightingale landed next to him. "Why are you sad? Your song is lovely." Ollie looked surprised. "But owls are supposed to hoot." "And nightingales are supposed to sing at night," said the nightingale. "Maybe you're a bit of both." Ollie practiced chirping songs with the nightingale every evening. Soon, his songs became beautiful and strange, unlike anything the forest had heard. One night, the forest animals held a talent show. The owls hooted, the frogs croaked and the crickets chirped. Then Ollie sang his unique owl song. The whole forest went silent, then burst into applause. Even the owls who had teased him clapped their wings. Ollie learned that being different can be your greatest gift.

## The Pencil That Wrote Itself | a magical pencil writing stories
Ivy found an old yellow pencil in her grandmother's desk drawer. When she set it on a piece of paper, it began to move by itself, writing a story about a girl who sailed to an island of talking birds. Ivy was amazed. Every night, the pencil wrote a new adventure. Ivy read them all and loved them. But one night, the pencil stopped in the middle of a story. The girl in the story was stuck in a cave, and the pencil didn't write any more. Ivy waited, but it didn't move. Finally, she picked it up herself and wrote what happened next: the girl found a glowing mushroom that lit the way out. When she put the pencil down, it started writing again, continuing from her words. Ivy realized the pencil wanted to share the story with her. From then on, they wrote together, taking turns. By the end of the year, they had filled ten notebooks, and Ivy had become a writer.

## The Cow Who Wanted to Be a Cat | a cow trying to be a cat
Daisy the cow lived on a farm and watched the farm cat, Whiskers, with great envy. Whiskers got to sleep inside on a soft cushion, drink milk from a bowl and get petted by the farmer's children. Daisy decided to act like a cat. She tried to curl up on a cushion, but she squashed it flat. She tried to climb a tree, but she only knocked off some apples. She tried to purr, but it came out as a loud moo. The children laughed so hard they fell over. Whiskers came over, rolling his eyes. "Daisy, you can't be a cat. But do you know what? The children ask for your milk every morning. They make cheese and butter from it, and they love it." Daisy hadn't thought of that. She looked at the happy children drinking milk at breakfast. She realized she already had something wonderful to give. From then on, she stopped trying to be a cat and was proud to be the best cow on the farm.

## The Great Pillow Fort | building a pillow fort with family
On a cold, rainy weekend, the power went out and the family was stuck at home. The kids, Ana and Tom, decided to build a pillow fort. They gathered every pillow, blanket and cushion in the house. Dad brought chairs for the walls. Mom brought clothespins to hold the blankets. Grandma contributed her biggest quilt for the roof. They built tunnels, a lookout tower and a secret room. Inside, they hung a flashlight from the ceiling and laid out snacks. The whole family crawled inside, even Grandpa, who got stuck in the tunnel and had to be pulled through, laughing. They told stories, played cards and ate popcorn. When the power came back on, nobody wanted to leave. They slept in the fort all night. The next morning, the living room was a disaster, but nobody minded. It became a family tradition: every time it rained hard, they built the Great Pillow Fort, and each year it got bigger.

## The Old Dog's Last Adventure | an old dog and a boy
Buster was an old dog with gray whiskers and stiff legs. He used to run all day, chasing balls and swimming in the lake. Now he mostly slept by the fire. Ten-year-old Liam missed playing with him. One sunny day, Liam had an idea. He put a blanket in his red wagon, gently lifted Buster into it and pulled him to all his favorite places. They went to the park where Buster used to chase squirrels. Buster's tail wagged at the familiar smells. They went to the lake, where Liam splashed water on Buster's paws, and Buster barked happily. They stopped at the bakery, where the baker gave Buster a treat, like she had done for years. At sunset, they sat on the hill behind the house, Buster's head in Liam's lap. "You're still the best dog in the world," Liam whispered. Buster licked his hand and closed his eyes, content. It was a perfect day, and Liam would remember it forever.

## The Girl Who Grew Wings | a dream of flying
One morning, eight-year-old Rosa woke up with small white wings on her back. At first, they were tiny and weak, and she could only flutter a few inches off the ground. Her family was amazed. Her friends at school were jealous. Rosa practiced flying every day. She jumped off her bed, then off the porch, then off the garden wall. She fell many times and scraped her knees. But slowly, her wings got stronger. One evening, she flapped hard and rose above the trees. She saw her whole town from the sky: the school, the river, the bakery and her own house with her parents waving from the yard. She flew until the stars came out. When she landed, she was tired but happy. The next morning, the wings were gone, as mysteriously as they had come. Rosa was sad at first. But she never forgot the view from the sky, and when she grew up, she became a pilot, flying above the clouds every day.

## The Recycling Robot | a robot sorting trash
In a city overflowing with trash, a small robot named Sorty worked at the recycling center. Its job was to sort plastic, paper, glass and metal into different bins. Sorty worked day and night, but there was always more trash. One day, a class of students visited the center. Sorty showed them how plastic bottles could become new clothes, how old paper became new notebooks and how glass could be melted and made into new jars. The students were amazed. They went back to school and started a recycling program. They put special bins in every classroom. They taught their families how to separate their trash at home. Soon, the trash arriving at the center was already sorted. Sorty had time to rest. One evening, the students came back to visit and brought Sorty a gift: a shiny new bin made from recycled metal, with "Thank you, Sorty!" painted on the side. Sorty's lights glowed green, its happiest color.

## The Firefly Who Lost Her Light | a firefly who can't glow
Lumi was a firefly who couldn't glow. All her friends lit up the summer nights with their bright yellow lights, dancing above the meadow. Lumi tried and tried, but her tail stayed dark. She felt left out and sad. One night, a boy came to the meadow with a jar, wanting to catch fireflies. The glowing fireflies were easy to spot, and he caught them one by one. Lumi, hidden in the dark, flew to the jar and pushed the lid open with all her strength. Her friends escaped and flew back to the meadow. The boy didn't even see her, because she was dark. The other fireflies were amazed. "You saved us because you don't glow!" they said. From then on, Lumi was the meadow's secret guardian. She watched over her friends from the shadows. And one night, when she was older, her tail flickered for the first time, a small soft glow, as if her bravery had finally lit her up from the inside.

## The Princess Who Wanted to Be an Engineer | a princess who builds
Princess Ruby didn't care for fancy dresses or royal balls. She loved building things. She built a pulley to lift her books to her tower room and a little water wheel in the castle fountain. The king and queen worried. "Princesses don't build machines," they said. One summer, the kingdom's old bridge over the river cracked, and nobody could cross to the market. The royal builders said it would take a year to fix. Ruby studied the bridge for three days. She drew plans for a stronger design using triangles, which she had read were the strongest shape. She worked with the builders, carrying planks and measuring angles. In just two months, the new bridge was finished, stronger than ever. The whole kingdom celebrated. The king and queen were proud. They gave Ruby her own workshop in the castle and named her Royal Engineer. She still went to the royal balls sometimes, but she always wore her favorite tool belt over her dress.

## The Gentle Tiger | a tiger who protects others
In a deep jungle lived a tiger named Raja, the strongest animal in the forest. All the animals were afraid of him. When he walked by, birds went silent and deer ran away. But Raja didn't want to be feared. He was lonely. One rainy season, a flood came and swept away the homes of many small animals. A family of monkeys was trapped on a floating log. Raja dove into the rushing water and pushed the log to shore. He carried baby deer on his back to high ground. He let the shivering rabbits sleep next to his warm fur. When the flood was over, the animals looked at Raja differently. They weren't afraid anymore. They brought him fruit, sang songs for him and invited him to their gatherings. Raja finally had friends. He was still the strongest animal in the jungle, but now he used his strength to protect, and that made him truly great.

## The Clever Farmer | a farmer outsmarting a greedy man
A greedy landowner told a poor farmer, "You can farm my land, but I'll take half of everything that grows. This year, I'll take whatever grows above the ground." The clever farmer planted potatoes. When harvest came, the landowner got only the leaves, and the farmer got all the potatoes underground. The landowner was furious. "Next year, I'll take everything that grows below the ground!" So the farmer planted rice. The landowner got only the roots, and the farmer got all the grain. The landowner was angrier than ever. "Next year, I'll take both the tops and the bottoms!" The farmer planted corn. The landowner got the roots and the tassels at the very top, while the farmer kept all the ears of corn in the middle. Finally, the landowner gave up and sold the land to the farmer for a fair price. The farmer shared his harvest with the whole village, and everyone laughed about the landowner who tried to be clever.

## The Silent Night Choir | a choir singing for a sick friend
Every December, the children's choir in town sang carols in the square. This year, their best singer, a girl named Joy, was in the hospital with a broken leg. She was sad to miss it. The choir decided to bring the concert to her. On the night of the performance, they walked through the snow to the hospital and gathered under her window. They lit candles and began to sing. The music floated up through the cold air. Nurses and patients came to their windows. A man in a wheelchair rolled outside to listen. Doctors on break stood in the doorway and smiled. Joy opened her window, wrapped in blankets, and sang along from her bed. When the last song ended, the whole hospital clapped. The choir decided to sing there every year after that, so that no one would have to spend the holidays without music, even if they couldn't go outside.

## The Puzzle With a Missing Piece | finishing a puzzle
Grandpa and Lin spent a whole week working on a giant puzzle of a mountain lake. Every evening after dinner, they sat together and fit the pieces in. They found the edges first, then the blue sky, then the green trees. Finally, only one piece was left to place. But they couldn't find it anywhere. They looked under the table, behind the couch and inside every box. Lin was disappointed. "It's ruined," she said. Grandpa looked at the puzzle and smiled. "Is it?" He took a piece of cardboard, cut it to the right shape and handed Lin her colored pencils. "Let's make our own piece." Lin carefully drew a tiny duck swimming on the lake, where the missing piece should be. It didn't match the rest exactly, but it was perfect. The duck became their favorite part of the puzzle. They framed it and hung it on the wall. Years later, Lin still smiles every time she sees the little hand-drawn duck.

## The Hungry Caterpillar's Lesson | a caterpillar's patience
A small green caterpillar named Kiko was always in a hurry. He ate leaves as fast as he could, because he wanted to become a butterfly right away. He watched the butterflies dancing in the air and felt impatient. "When will it be my turn?" he asked every day. A wise old ladybug told him, "Everything happens when it's ready." But Kiko didn't want to wait. When the time came to make his chrysalis, he wrapped himself up and tried to hurry, wiggling and pushing. But nothing happened faster. So he had no choice but to wait, quietly, in the dark. Days passed. Kiko learned to be still and to trust that something was changing inside him. When he finally pushed his way out, he unfolded the most beautiful orange and black wings. He floated up into the sunshine. The ladybug waved. Kiko smiled down at her. He understood now. The waiting was part of the change.
