## The Block Builder's Bridge | building a bridge in a block world
In a world made entirely of square blocks, a young builder named Kip wanted to cross a wide canyon to reach a forest full of rare flowers. He had only a small stack of wooden blocks. He placed them one by one over the edge, very carefully, but he ran out halfway. Kip didn't give up. He went back, chopped more trees, and came back with more wood. He placed torches along the bridge so it would be safe at night. When he finally reached the other side, he discovered that the forest was home to a village of builders who had been trying to cross from their side for years. They had been building their own half-bridge, which was just a few blocks away from his! Kip placed the final block, and the two halves met in the middle. The villagers cheered, and that night they held a feast on the bridge. Kip learned that sometimes, when you build toward others, they're already building toward you.

## The Snail Mail Friend | slow letters between friends
Pia and her grandfather lived in different countries, and they wrote real letters to each other on paper. Pia's friends thought this was silly. "Why don't you just send a message on a phone?" they asked. But Pia loved the letters. She loved the stamps with pictures of birds, the smell of Grandpa's paper and the way he drew tiny doodles in the margins. Each letter took two weeks to arrive. Waiting for it was part of the fun. Once, Grandpa mailed her a pressed flower from his garden. Another time, he sent a riddle and made her wait for the next letter to find the answer. When Pia was sad, she reread his old letters, which she kept in a shoebox. Years later, when Grandpa was very old, Pia visited him and found that he had kept every single letter she had ever written, in a box exactly like hers. They spent the whole afternoon reading them together, laughing at Pia's first wobbly handwriting.

## The Volcano Island | explorers on a volcano island
Three young explorers, Ari, Lena and Max, sailed with their science teacher to a small volcanic island to study rocks. The island was black and rocky, with steaming vents and strange, bubbling mud pools. Their teacher explained that the island had formed from lava that cooled and hardened, layer by layer, over thousands of years. Ari found a light, bubbly rock called pumice that floated in water. Lena found shiny black glass called obsidian. Max collected a jar of warm sand. As they explored, the ground trembled slightly, and the teacher checked her instruments. "Just a small rumble," she said, "but we'll head back to the boat to be safe." From the deck, they watched a puff of gray smoke rise from the top of the volcano. Nobody was in danger, but it reminded them that the Earth is alive and always changing. Back at school, they built a display of their rocks, and Max's jar of sand was labeled "the newest land on Earth."

## The Shoemaker and the Tiny Helpers | elves helping a shoemaker
A poor shoemaker had only enough leather left to make one last pair of shoes. He cut the pieces in the evening and went to bed, worried about the future. In the morning, he found a perfect pair of shoes on his workbench, stitched more beautifully than he had ever done himself. A customer bought them for a high price. With the money, the shoemaker bought leather for two pairs. Again, he cut them at night, and in the morning, they were finished. This happened night after night, and soon his shop was famous. Curious, he and his wife hid one night and watched. Two tiny elves in ragged clothes climbed onto the bench and sewed all night long. The shoemaker's wife sewed tiny warm clothes for them, and the shoemaker made them two pairs of tiny boots. When the elves found the gifts, they danced with joy and sang. They never came back after that, but the shoemaker never forgot their kindness and always helped anyone in need.

## The Kid Who Couldn't Whistle | learning to whistle
Everyone in Tom's family could whistle. His dad whistled songs while cooking. His mom whistled for the dog. Even his little sister could whistle, though only one note. Tom puckered his lips, blew and blew, and nothing came out but air. He practiced in the bathroom, in the garden and on the bus. People stared. He watched videos and read instructions. Lips round. Tongue low. Blow gently. Still nothing. After two months, he almost gave up. Then, one evening, while he was brushing his teeth, a tiny squeak came out. Tom froze. He tried again. A real note! It was thin and wobbly, but it was a whistle. He ran through the house, whistling his one note over and over. His family clapped and cheered like he had won a gold medal. Within a week, Tom could whistle a whole song. He learned that some things take a long time, and that the people who love you will cheer the loudest when you finally get there.

## The Sloth Who Won the Race | a sloth in a race
When the animals of the rainforest held a race, everyone laughed when Sid the sloth signed up. Sloths are famously slow. The race started at dawn. The jaguar, the monkey and the toucan rushed ahead. Sid moved one arm, then the other, very slowly along a vine. The jaguar ran so fast that it didn't notice the river and had to swim across, getting soaked and tired. The monkey swung so wildly that it got tangled in vines. The toucan flew in circles because it forgot the way. Meanwhile, Sid kept moving, slow and steady, along the vines that went straight over the river, past the tangled trees and toward the finish line. It took him all day. By the time he arrived at sunset, all the other animals were exhausted and resting on the ground. Sid touched the finish line first, then promptly fell asleep. When he woke up three days later, he had a trophy made of leaves waiting next to him.

## The Toy Soldier's Journey | a toy soldier lost in a city
A small tin soldier fell out of a boy's pocket on a busy city street. He lay in the gutter as cars rushed past and people stepped over him. Then it started to rain, and the water carried him into a drain. Down below, he floated through dark tunnels, past curious rats and dripping pipes. He wasn't scared, because he was a soldier, and soldiers stand tall. Finally, the water spilled out into a river, and a fish swallowed him whole! Inside the fish, it was dark and slimy. The fish was caught by a fisherman, sold at the market and bought by a cook. When she cut the fish open, she found the little tin soldier inside. The cook's son recognized him at once. "That's my soldier! I lost him last week!" The boy put the soldier on his shelf next to his other toys. The tin soldier stood a little taller than before, because now he had the best adventure story of all.

## The Night the Stars Fell | a meteor shower
Every August, Grandpa took Nadia to the hill behind the village to watch the meteor shower. They lay on a blanket and looked up at the dark sky. "Meteors are little bits of space rock and dust," Grandpa explained. "When Earth passes through a trail of dust left by a comet, the bits burn up in our air, and we see shooting stars." That year, Nadia counted fifty-seven meteors. Each time, she made a wish. Some meteors were quick little streaks. Others were long and bright, leaving a glowing trail. One was so big that it flashed green before disappearing. Nadia gasped. "That was the best one!" Grandpa laughed. "In my whole life, I've only seen three green ones." Many years later, when Grandpa was gone, Nadia brought her own daughter to the same hill every August. She told her about the space dust, the comet trails and the green meteor. And every year, she made one wish for Grandpa, wherever he was.

## The Overly Polite Robot | a robot that is too polite
Bertrand the butler robot was programmed to be polite, maybe a little too polite. When the family's son asked for a glass of water, Bertrand bowed and said, "Certainly, young sir, it would be my greatest honor and deepest pleasure to fetch you a refreshing glass of water at once." By the time he finished, the boy had already gotten it himself. When the mother said, "Thanks, Bertrand," the robot replied with a five-minute speech about gratitude. When the cat knocked over a vase, Bertrand apologized to the cat. The family loved Bertrand, but his politeness was making everything take forever. The youngest daughter decided to help. She taught him a new rule: "Short and kind is best." Now when someone thanks him, he says, "You're welcome!" and smiles his little screen smile. He still apologizes to the cat sometimes, though. Old habits are hard to break, even for robots.

## The Mango Tree's Promise | a mango tree and a child
When Dao was born, her father planted a mango tree in their yard. "You'll grow up together," he said. Every year, Dao measured herself against the tree. When she was three, the tree was taller. When she was six, it had its first flowers. When she was eight, it gave its first sweet mangoes, and Dao ate one every evening on the porch with her father. When Dao was twelve, a storm broke one of the tree's big branches. Dao cried, worried the tree would die. Her father showed her how to clean the wound and protect it. The next year, new branches grew stronger than before. When Dao grew up and moved to the city for university, she missed the tree most of all. Every summer, she came home, and her father would hand her the first ripe mango of the season, saved just for her. Years later, when Dao had her own daughter, she planted a mango tree in her own yard, and made the same promise.

## The Missing Melody | a musician who loses a song
Felix was a street musician who played the violin in the town square. One morning, he woke up and couldn't remember his favorite song, the one his grandmother had taught him. He tried to play it, but the notes came out wrong. He searched his memory, but the melody was gone. Felix felt lost. He walked around town, hoping something would remind him. He heard birds singing, a baker humming and children chanting a jump-rope rhyme. None of them were his song. Then he passed an old woman sitting on a bench, feeding pigeons, and she was quietly singing to herself. It was his grandmother's song! Felix sat down beside her. She told him she had heard it years ago from a young violinist in the square, a boy who played every morning. Felix realized she had heard him play it as a child. She had kept his song safe all these years. He played it for her right there, and she sang along.

## The Polar Bear and the Ice | a polar bear and melting ice
On the Arctic ice lived a young polar bear named Nanu. He loved sliding on the ice and diving for seals. But each year, he noticed that the ice melted earlier in spring and froze later in autumn. His mother had to swim farther to find food. "Why is the ice shrinking?" Nanu asked. A wise old walrus explained, "The world is getting warmer because people burn too much fuel that heats the air. The ice is melting faster than it used to." Nanu felt worried. "Can anything be done?" The walrus pointed toward the faraway lights of a research station. "The humans are starting to understand. Some are working hard to use cleaner energy and protect places like ours." That summer, a team of scientists visited the ice to study it. A young scientist saw Nanu and his mother from a distance. She took a photo and shared it with schools around the world, asking kids to help protect the Arctic. Nanu didn't know it, but children everywhere were now thinking about him.

## The Garage Band | kids starting a band
Four friends decided to start a band in Leo's garage. Leo played drums on pots and pans. Mia had an old guitar with only five strings. Sam played a keyboard that was missing two keys. Zoe sang, though she was too shy to sing louder than a whisper. Their first practice was a disaster. The pots were too loud, the guitar was out of tune and nobody could hear Zoe. The neighbors closed their windows. But they kept practicing every Saturday. Leo learned to play softer. Mia's uncle fixed her guitar. Sam learned songs that didn't need the missing keys. And slowly, Zoe started singing louder. After three months, they performed at the school talent show. They were nervous, and Leo dropped a drumstick, but they kept playing. When Zoe sang the last chorus in her full voice, the whole audience stood up and clapped. They didn't win first place, but the principal asked them to play at the end-of-year party. The Garage Band was officially famous, at least at their school.

## The Wandering Compass | a compass that points to what you need
In an antique shop, a girl named Ella found an old brass compass. It didn't point north. Instead, its needle spun and settled in a strange direction. The shopkeeper smiled. "That compass points to whatever you need most." Ella bought it and followed it out of the shop. It led her to the park, where a boy was sitting alone on a swing, crying. He had just moved to the town and had no friends. Ella sat next to him, and they talked until the streetlights came on. The next day, the compass pointed to the library, where Ella found the exact book she needed for her school project. Another day, it pointed home, where her mom was sick and needed help with dinner. Ella began to understand. The compass didn't show her treasure. It showed her where she could do good or find something important. Years later, she gave the compass to her son, and told him, "Trust it, but also learn to feel where you're needed on your own."

## The Kitten's First Snow | a kitten seeing snow
Mittens was a kitten who had been born in the summer, so she had never seen snow. One morning, she woke up and looked out the window. The whole garden was covered in something white and sparkly. Mittens pressed her nose against the glass. Was it milk? Feathers? Clouds that had fallen? When her owner opened the door, Mittens took one careful step outside. Her paw sank into the cold, soft snow, and she jumped back in surprise. Then she tried again. She pounced on a snowflake and watched it disappear on her nose. She chased a bird's footprints across the yard. She rolled, sneezed and pounced until her whiskers were frosty and white. When she came back inside, her owner wrapped her in a warm towel and set her by the heater. Mittens curled up, purring and dreaming of snow. From then on, every morning in winter, she sat by the window waiting, hoping the sky would give her more of that cold, magical stuff.

## The Grandmother Who Climbed a Mountain | an old woman achieving a dream
Grandma Rosa was seventy-five years old, and she had always dreamed of climbing to the top of the mountain near her village. When she was young, she was too busy working. When she was older, people told her she was too old. On her seventy-fifth birthday, she decided she was done waiting. She started training. She walked every morning, first around the block, then up the hill to the temple, then along the river trail. Her grandchildren joined her. After six months, she was ready. The whole family set off at dawn. The path was steep, and they stopped often to rest and drink water. Grandma Rosa's knees ached, but she kept going, one step at a time. At noon, they reached the summit. The whole valley spread out below them, green and golden. Grandma Rosa sat on a rock and cried happy tears. "Dreams don't have an expiration date," she told her grandchildren. "You just have to start walking."

## The Lost Kite Over the City | a kite flying away over a city
During the kite festival, a gust of wind snapped the string of Jin's dragon kite. It soared away over the rooftops of the city. Jin ran after it, through alleys and across bridges, keeping his eyes on the red dragon in the sky. He ran past a market, where a fruit seller pointed. "It went that way!" He ran past a school, where children waved from the windows. A bus driver slowed down to let him cross. A policeman on a bicycle helped him follow it. Finally, the kite got tangled on the antenna of a tall apartment building. Jin looked up, out of breath. An old woman on the top floor saw it, reached out of her window with a broom and gently unhooked it. She came down the stairs and handed it to him. Jin thanked her and all the people who had helped him chase it. He realized his kite hadn't been lost at all. It had just shown him how many kind people lived in his city.

## The Hermit Crab's New Home | a hermit crab finding a shell
Hermy the hermit crab had outgrown his shell. It squeezed his legs and pinched his back. He needed a new home, but finding the right shell is very hard for a hermit crab. He tried a big snail shell, but it was so heavy he couldn't walk. He tried a tiny spiral shell, but only his head fit inside. He tried a plastic bottle cap, but it was ugly and hot in the sun. Hermy was tired and discouraged. Then he met another hermit crab, a little one named Shelly, who was also looking for a home. Hermy had an idea. "My old shell is too small for me, but it might be perfect for you." He gave it to her, and it fit Shelly exactly. Shelly was so grateful that she told him about a beautiful empty shell she had seen near the tide pools. Hermy found it, and it was perfect. The two crabs became neighbors, and they promised to always help each other find new homes as they grew.

## The Tired Teacher | students surprising their teacher
Ms. Nida had been teaching for thirty years, and she was tired. She stayed late every day grading papers and came early to prepare lessons. Her students noticed that she yawned a lot and didn't smile as much as before. The class decided to surprise her. They came to school early one morning, before Ms. Nida arrived. They cleaned the classroom until it sparkled. They decorated the board with drawings and wrote "We love you, Ms. Nida!" in giant letters. Each student wrote a card about something she had taught them. One boy wrote that she taught him to love reading. A girl wrote that Ms. Nida had believed in her when nobody else did. When Ms. Nida walked in, she stopped in the doorway. She read every card, one by one, while the class watched. By the end, she was laughing and crying at the same time. "Thirty years," she said, "and this is the best day of all of them."

## The Big Little Lie | a small lie that grows
Kiet broke his mom's favorite vase while playing ball inside the house. He panicked and hid the pieces under the couch. When his mom asked what happened, he said, "I don't know. Maybe the cat did it." His mom frowned at the cat. Kiet felt a little sick inside. The next day, his mom took the cat to the vet to check if it was hurt. Kiet felt worse. His mom told the neighbors to watch their vases because the cat had gotten clumsy. Kiet felt even worse. The lie kept growing, like a balloon getting bigger and bigger. That night, Kiet couldn't sleep. He went to his mom's room and told her the truth. He waited for her to be angry. She sighed and hugged him. "I'm upset about the vase," she said, "but I'm prouder that you told me the truth. A lie always gets heavier the longer you carry it." The next morning, Kiet gave the cat an extra treat and apologized. The cat didn't seem to mind at all.

## The Upside-Down Umbrella | an umbrella that collects things
On a windy, rainy day, Lily's umbrella flipped inside out. Instead of keeping her dry, it became a bowl. Lily laughed and held it up. It caught raindrops, then a falling leaf, then a lost butterfly that needed a rest. Lily walked through town with her upside-down umbrella. A little bird hopped in to drink the rainwater. A dandelion seed floated in. A boy dropped a paper note into it as a joke: "Hello, umbrella girl!" By the time she got home, her umbrella held a tiny collection of treasures from her walk. She set the butterfly free on a flower, let the bird finish its drink and kept the note in her diary. Her mom offered to buy her a new umbrella. "No thanks," said Lily. "This one sees the world differently." From then on, on rainy days, Lily always carried her umbrella the right way, but on sunny, windy days, she flipped it over just to see what the sky would give her.

## The First Day of School | a nervous first day
Nong Mint was starting at a new school, and her stomach was full of butterflies. She didn't know anyone. What if nobody talked to her? What if she got lost? Her mom kissed her goodbye at the gate, and Mint walked in slowly, holding her backpack straps tightly. In the classroom, she sat at an empty desk by the window. A girl with two braids sat next to her and said, "I like your pencil case. Is that a cat?" It was. Mint smiled. "Her name is Mochi," she said. The girl's name was Fai, and she had a cat too. At lunch, Fai introduced Mint to her friends. In art class, they painted together. In P.E., they were on the same team. When Mint's mom picked her up, she asked, "How was it?" Mint grinned. "I have a best friend now." Her mom laughed. "Already?" Mint nodded. "It only took one sentence," she said, "about a cat pencil case."

## The Robot Who Wanted to Dream | a robot that wants to dream
Every night, when the house went quiet, the family's robot assistant, Nova, entered sleep mode. But Nova didn't dream. It just shut off and turned back on in the morning. The little girl of the family, Kai, told Nova about her dreams every morning: flying over oceans, talking to dolphins, eating cake on the moon. Nova was curious. "What is it like to dream?" it asked. Kai thought. "It's like your brain tells you stories while you sleep." So that night, before entering sleep mode, Nova did something new. It took all the stories Kai had told it, mixed them together and played them back in its memory, slowly, like a movie. It imagined flying dolphins eating cake on the ocean. In the morning, Nova told Kai about its first dream. She laughed and clapped. "That's a great dream!" Nova didn't know if it had really dreamed. But it knew it had enjoyed it, and that seemed like the most important part.
