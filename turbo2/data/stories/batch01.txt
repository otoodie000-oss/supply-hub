## The Lighthouse Keeper's Cat | a lighthouse cat
On a rocky island far from the city stood a tall white lighthouse, and in it lived an old keeper named Mr. Pell and his gray cat, Pebble. Every night Mr. Pell climbed the spiral stairs to light the great lamp, and every night Pebble followed, counting the steps with her soft paws. One stormy evening, Mr. Pell slipped on the wet stairs and hurt his ankle. He could not climb. Rain hammered the windows, and far out at sea a little fishing boat was looking for the safe way home. Pebble looked at the keeper, then at the stairs. She ran all the way to the top, where a long rope hung from the old bell. She jumped, grabbed the rope with her claws, and swung. Clang! Clang! Clang! The fishermen heard the bell and steered away from the rocks. When the storm cleared, they rowed to the island to say thank you. Mr. Pell laughed and pointed at the sleepy cat curled on his lap. From that day on, the fishermen left a small fish on the dock every Sunday, just for Pebble.

## The Upside-Down Day | a day when everything was upside down
When Nina woke up on Tuesday, her bed was on the ceiling. So was her dresser, her rug, and her goldfish bowl, which somehow kept all its water. Nina had to climb down her bookshelf like a ladder. In the kitchen, her dad was eating cereal that floated up instead of down, and he caught each flake with his spoon in the air. "It's an upside-down day," he said, as if this happened every month. At school, the teacher wrote on the floor, and everyone sat on the walls. Nina noticed that being upside down made the hard math problems look easy, because she was seeing them from a new side. At recess, the kids jumped off the swings and fell slowly toward the clouds, laughing. By bedtime, everything flipped back with a gentle thump. Nina wrote in her diary: "Today I learned that if something seems hard, try looking at it from a different direction."

## Pip and the Paper Boat | a paper boat on a river
Pip was a small paper boat folded by a girl named Lan on a rainy afternoon. She set him in the gutter, and the water carried him away. Pip sailed past a sleeping dog, under a bridge made of a fallen branch, and through a puddle so wide it felt like an ocean. A frog hopped aboard and asked where he was going. "I don't know," said Pip, "but I'm going there fast." The gutter flowed into a stream, and the stream flowed into a pond in the park. There, Pip slowed down and floated in a circle while ducks swam around him. His paper was getting soft, and he worried he would sink. Then a small hand lifted him out of the water. It was Lan! She had followed him all the way, running along the street. She dried him on a warm rock in the sun. That night, Pip sat on her windowsill, a little wrinkled, but proud of the biggest adventure a paper boat could ever have.

## The Robot Who Collected Sounds | a robot collecting sounds
In a quiet repair shop lived a small robot named Echo. Echo did not collect stamps or coins. Echo collected sounds. Each morning, Echo rolled out with a tiny microphone and recorded whatever it could find: the crunch of autumn leaves, the hiss of a bicycle tire, the buzz of a bee near the flowers. At night, Echo played them back for the old shopkeeper, Mrs. Ruiz, who smiled and named each one. One day, Echo heard a sound it could not name. It was soft, broken, and came from behind the bakery. Echo followed it and found a little boy crying because he had lost his kite. Echo played the boy its happiest sounds: a kitten's purr, a song from a passing radio, and the laughter of children at the playground. The boy began to smile. Together they found the kite stuck in a tree, and Echo stretched its arm to bring it down. That night Echo added a new sound to its collection, a boy saying "thank you," and it became Echo's favorite of all.

## Grandma's Secret Recipe | a secret family recipe
Every year, Grandma Ploy made her famous coconut soup for the family festival, and every year nobody knew her secret ingredient. This year Grandma had a cold, so she asked her grandson Tawan to cook. "But I don't know the secret!" he said. Grandma just winked and handed him her old notebook. Tawan read every page. There were notes about lemongrass, galangal, lime leaves, and coconut milk, but no secret. He chopped and stirred and tasted, and the soup was good, but something was missing. Then he found the last page. It said only: "Cook it slowly, and think about the people who will eat it." Tawan laughed. He turned down the heat and thought about his little cousin who loved the mushrooms, his uncle who always asked for extra lime, and Grandma, smiling in her chair. When the family tasted the soup that night, everyone said it was the best ever. Grandma whispered, "Now you know the secret too."

## The Shy Volcano | a shy volcano
Mount Tumble was a young volcano who was very, very shy. The older volcanoes rumbled and puffed great clouds of smoke, showing off to the sky. Tumble only made a tiny squeak of steam whenever someone looked at him. The birds teased him, and the clouds drifted past without noticing. One winter, the valley below grew terribly cold. The animals shivered, and the river froze solid. A wise old tortoise climbed up Tumble's slope and said, "Your warmth is not for showing off. It's for sharing." Tumble thought about this. He did not want to explode or make a big scene. Instead, he let a gentle warmth flow through the rocks, and soon hot springs bubbled up in the valley. The animals gathered in the warm water, and the river began to melt. That night, the birds sang a thank-you song for Tumble. He blushed so much that his top glowed a soft orange, and for once, he didn't mind being seen.

## The Library After Dark | a library at night
When the town library closed for the night, the books came alive. The dictionary yawned and stretched its pages. The atlas unfolded its maps and argued with the globe about where the mountains were. The poetry books whispered rhymes to each other in the corner. One night, a new book arrived. It was thin, with a plain cover and no pictures, and it was too nervous to say anything. "What kind of book are you?" asked the big encyclopedia. "I'm a notebook," said the new book quietly. "My pages are empty." The other books laughed, but the old storybook hushed them. "Empty pages are the most exciting kind," she said. "They hold stories nobody has written yet." The next morning, a girl checked out the notebook and began writing her very first story inside it. By the end of the summer, every page was full, and the notebook came back to the library proud and bursting with ideas.

## Max and the Missing Socks | missing socks
Max had a problem. Every time his family did laundry, one sock disappeared. Never a pair, always just one. His mom blamed the washing machine. His sister blamed the dog. Max decided to solve the mystery. He set up a small camera made from an old phone and pointed it at the laundry basket. The next morning, he watched the video with his heart pounding. At midnight, the basket wobbled. A tiny paw reached out, grabbed a blue striped sock, and pulled it away. Max followed the clues: a trail of lint, a sock thread on the stairs, and finally a little hole behind the bookshelf. Inside, he found a family of mice sleeping in a nest made entirely of colorful socks. The baby mice were snuggled in his favorite soccer sock. Max didn't have the heart to take it back. Instead, he gave them a whole old pillowcase and asked them, very politely, to leave his new socks alone. Strangely enough, they did.

## The Star Who Wanted to Visit | a star who visits earth
High above the world, a small star named Lumi watched the children on Earth every night. They pointed at the sky and made wishes, and Lumi wished she could see their world up close. One night, she gathered all her courage and slid down a moonbeam. She landed softly in a field of tall grass. Everything was enormous and surprising. Fireflies thought she was their queen. A cricket played her a song. A sleepy farmer saw the glow and thought it was a lantern left on. Lumi spent the whole night exploring, but as dawn came, she began to fade. Stars need the dark sky to shine. A little boy named Somchai saw her flickering by the fence. "You need to go home," he said gently, and he held up a mirror to catch the last moonlight. Lumi rode the reflection back into the sky. Now, every night, one star twinkles a little brighter above Somchai's house, and he always waves back.

## The Grumpy Garden Gnome | a grumpy garden gnome
Bartholomew was a garden gnome who did not like anything. He did not like the rain, because it made his hat drip. He did not like the sun, because it made his paint peel. Most of all, he did not like the noisy children next door who played ball near his flower bed. One afternoon, the ball rolled right up to him and knocked him over. Bartholomew lay in the dirt, grumbling, until a small girl picked him up and cleaned his face with her sleeve. "Sorry, Mr. Gnome," she said. "You look lonely out here." She brought him a tiny blanket made from a napkin and placed a daisy next to him. The next day, the children came back with a little painted bench and a toy cat to keep him company. Bartholomew still grumbled about the rain and the sun. But if you looked very closely, you might notice that the corners of his painted mouth had turned up, just a little, into a smile.

## The Kite That Was Afraid of Heights | a kite afraid of heights
Zippy was a bright red kite with a long yellow tail, and he had a secret: he was afraid of heights. Whenever his owner, Mia, ran across the field, Zippy flapped and tumbled and fell back to the grass on purpose. "Maybe it's too windy," Mia said, or "Maybe it's not windy enough." One day, an old blue kite stuck in a tree called down to him. "What are you afraid of?" "Falling," whispered Zippy. The old kite laughed kindly. "But you have Mia holding the string. You can't fly away and you can't fall far. That string is how you stay safe." The next time Mia ran, Zippy felt the string tug gently, like a hand holding his. He rose a little, then a little more. The air was cool and the clouds were close, and the whole park looked like a green blanket. Zippy flew higher than any kite that day, and he wasn't afraid at all, because he knew someone was holding on.

## The Snail Race | a snail race
Every spring, the garden held a great snail race from the watering can to the big rock, a distance of nearly two whole meters. Speedy Sam had won three years in a row. He trained by sliding down wet leaves and eating extra lettuce. This year, a small new snail named Olive entered the race. Everyone giggled because Olive stopped to look at everything: a shiny pebble, a beetle, a raindrop on a petal. When the race began, Sam zoomed ahead. Olive went slowly, greeting the ants along the path. Halfway there, Sam took a shortcut under a flower pot and got stuck in a sticky puddle of spilled jam. Olive found him and helped push him out. They were both so covered in jam that they started laughing. They crossed the finish line together, sticky and happy, long after sunset. The judges, two very patient ladybugs, declared it the first tie in garden history, and both snails got a trophy made from a bottle cap.

## The Little Cloud | a little cloud that wanted to rain
Puff was the smallest cloud in the sky, and she wanted more than anything to make rain. The big gray clouds rumbled and poured rain on the farms, and the farmers cheered. But whenever Puff tried, only one tiny drop fell out, and it usually landed on a confused pigeon. "You're too small," said the thunderclouds. Puff drifted away sadly until she floated over a little balcony in the city. There, a boy was trying to grow a single sunflower in a pot, and its leaves were drooping in the heat. Puff squeezed with all her might. One drop, two drops, ten drops fell, a perfect little shower just the size of the pot. The sunflower lifted its head. The boy looked up and saw the tiny cloud. "Thank you!" he shouted. Puff realized that not every job needs to be big. Some things only need a little help, at exactly the right moment, from exactly the right size of friend.

## Captain Crumb's Treasure | a pirate who loved cookies
Captain Crumb was the most feared pirate on the Sugar Sea, though not for the usual reasons. He didn't want gold or jewels. He wanted cookies. His ship, the Crunchy Wave, sailed from island to island looking for the legendary Golden Cookie, said to be the most delicious treat ever baked. His crew was tired of eating crumbs for dinner. After many weeks, they found an old map in a bottle. It led them to a tiny island with a single bakery run by a cheerful old woman named Nana Rosa. "The Golden Cookie?" she said. "It's just my regular recipe, baked with friends." She invited the whole crew inside, and they spent the afternoon mixing dough, cracking eggs and laughing. When the cookies came out of the oven, warm and golden, Captain Crumb took a bite and closed his eyes. "This is it," he said. "The treasure was the baking all along." From then on, the Crunchy Wave became the only pirate ship with a full kitchen.

## The Boy Who Planted a Forest | planting trees
When Arun was eight, his village was dusty and hot because the trees had been cut down long ago. Arun's grandfather told him stories about the forest that used to grow there, full of birds and cool shade. So Arun planted one tree. Every day he carried water from the well in a small bucket. The other children laughed. "One tree won't make a forest!" Arun planted another, and another. After a year, his friend Devi started helping. Then her brothers joined. Then the teacher brought the whole class. They collected seeds, built little fences to protect the saplings, and took turns watering. Years passed. The trees grew tall, and birds returned to build nests. The village became cooler, and a small stream began to flow again. When Arun was grown up, the children of the village asked him how the forest began. He smiled and said, "With one tree, and a lot of friends who believed it could grow."

## The Clock That Ran Backwards | a clock that ran backwards
In the corner of Mia's grandmother's house stood a tall wooden clock that ran backwards. Tick, tock, the hands went the wrong way. Grandma said it had always been like that. One rainy afternoon, Mia touched the clock's golden pendulum, and suddenly the room shimmered. The flowers on the table un-wilted. Her half-eaten sandwich became whole again. Mia gasped. The clock was turning back time in the house! At first it was fun. She un-spilled her juice and un-broke a cookie. But then she noticed Grandma's paintings becoming blank canvases, and the photos on the wall showing a younger Grandma, then no Grandma at all. Mia realized that going backwards also meant losing good memories. She stopped the pendulum with both hands. Everything settled. That evening, Grandma painted a new picture of Mia beside the clock. "Time is like a story," Grandma said. "The best part is always what we make next."

## The Penguin Who Loved Sunshine | a penguin who loved warm weather
Pablo was a penguin who lived in Antarctica, where everything was white, cold and windy. All the other penguins loved diving into the icy water. Pablo loved sunshine. He would find the one sunny spot on the ice and lie on his back with his flippers spread wide. "You're a strange penguin," his friends said. One day, a scientist's ship arrived, and Pablo saw a poster inside the cabin. It showed a sandy beach with palm trees. Pablo stared at it for hours. The scientists noticed, and they laughed and took a photo of him. Pablo didn't go to the beach that year, or ever. But the scientists gave him the poster, and he set it up in his favorite sunny spot. Every evening he sat in front of it and imagined the warm sand. Sometimes, being happy isn't about going somewhere new. Sometimes it's about bringing a little bit of your dream to where you already are.

## The Night the Power Went Out | a blackout at home
One summer night, there was a loud pop and every light in the apartment went dark. The fan stopped. The TV went silent. Six-year-old Fah grabbed her mother's arm. "Don't worry," said Mom, "it's just a power cut." She found some candles and a flashlight, and the family gathered in the living room. Without the TV, it was very quiet. Then Dad made a shadow rabbit on the wall with his hands. Fah made a shadow bird. Her big brother made a shadow dinosaur that ate the bird, and everyone laughed. Grandpa told a story about when he was a boy and there was no electricity in his village at all. They opened the window and heard frogs singing outside and saw more stars than usual, because the whole street was dark. When the lights finally came back on, everyone groaned. "Can we turn them off again?" Fah asked. The next Saturday, the family made it a new tradition: one evening with no screens, just candles, shadows and stories.

## The Bee Who Couldn't Buzz | a bee who couldn't buzz
In a busy hive lived a little bee named Bea who couldn't buzz. When she flapped her wings, they made no sound at all. The other bees talked about her. "How will the flowers know she's coming?" they whispered. Bea worked hard anyway. She was quiet, so she was very good at listening. One day, while collecting nectar in a meadow, she heard a faint crackling. It was smoke from a small fire near the edge of the woods. The other bees were buzzing too loudly to notice. Bea raced back to the hive and danced the warning dance as fast as she could. The bees understood and moved the queen and the honey to safety. The farmer saw the smoke too and put out the fire. That evening, the queen called Bea forward. "We were all so busy making noise," she said, "that only the quiet one heard the danger." From then on, Bea was the hive's official listener, and she never wished to buzz again.

## Lost in the Museum | getting lost in a museum
On a school trip to the natural history museum, Kai stopped to look at a giant squid in a glass tank and when he turned around, his class was gone. The halls were enormous and echoey. He walked past a whale skeleton hanging from the ceiling, a room full of glittering rocks, and a dark tunnel that showed the inside of a cave. He tried to remember what his teacher had said: "If you get lost, find someone in a museum uniform." Kai saw a woman with a name badge near the dinosaur hall. He walked up and told her his name and his school. She smiled and spoke into her radio. While they waited, she showed him a secret drawer of fossils that visitors could touch. Kai held a real trilobite, older than the dinosaurs. When his teacher arrived, looking very relieved, Kai was busy explaining trilobites to the museum guard. "Next time," his teacher said, "please stay with the group. But I'm glad you were smart and asked for help."

## The Seed That Waited | patience and a seed
In a dry corner of a garden lay a small brown seed. When spring came, the other seeds sprouted quickly. Tulips pushed up green leaves, and beans climbed their poles. But the little seed stayed underground and did nothing. "Hurry up," said a beetle. "Everyone else is growing." The seed felt worried, but she waited, because something told her it wasn't time yet. Summer came, hot and dry. Many of the fast plants drooped and faded. Then, at last, autumn rain soaked the soil, and the seed cracked open. She sent down deep roots and pushed up a sturdy stem. By winter, she had grown into a small evergreen tree, strong enough to stay green in the frost when every other plant in the garden was asleep. The beetle, shivering, took shelter under her branches. "I'm sorry I rushed you," he said. The little tree rustled gently. "Everyone grows in their own time," she said.

## The Talking Backpack | a talking backpack
On the first day of fourth grade, Leo's new backpack said, "Good morning!" Leo nearly dropped it. The backpack's name was Zip, and it could only talk to Leo. At first Zip was very helpful. "You forgot your math book," it would say, or "Your lunch is in the front pocket." But Zip also gave opinions. "That sandwich looks boring," it said. "You should sit with the kids playing chess." Leo was nervous, but he sat down at the chess table anyway. A girl named Ava taught him how the knight moves in an L shape. By the end of the week, Leo had two new friends and a chess club. One day, Zip went quiet. Leo shook it, opened every pocket, and called its name. Nothing. That evening, he found a note inside: "You don't need me anymore. Good luck, Leo." Leo smiled and packed his chess set. He missed Zip, but he knew the backpack was right.

## The Ocean in a Jar | a jar of ocean water
Mai lived in a city far from the sea. Her uncle, who was a sailor, sent her a glass jar filled with ocean water for her birthday. "When you miss the sea, listen," said his note. Mai held the jar to her ear and heard the soft rush of waves. Every night she placed the jar by her bed, and she dreamed of dolphins, coral reefs and wide blue horizons. One day, her class learned that plastic in the ocean hurts sea turtles and fish. Mai looked at her jar and felt worried for the real sea. She asked her teacher if the class could help. Together they collected plastic bottles at school and sent them to be recycled. They wrote letters to a beach cleaning group and raised money with a bake sale. At the end of the year, Mai's uncle visited and took the whole class to the coast. Mai poured her jar of ocean water back into the sea. "Thank you for the dreams," she whispered, and a wave splashed her toes as if to say you're welcome.

## The Mountain Goat Who Wanted to Fly | a goat who wanted wings
High on a rocky cliff lived a young mountain goat named Tilly, who watched the eagles soar and wished she could fly. She tried flapping her ears. She tried jumping off small rocks with leaves tied to her legs. She always landed with a bump. "Goats don't fly," said her mother kindly. One day, an eagle chick fell from its nest onto a narrow ledge far below. The mother eagle circled, but the ledge was too narrow for her wide wings. Tilly looked at the steep rocks. She was a mountain goat, and mountain goats are the best climbers in the world. Step by careful step, she hopped down the cliff, gently nudged the chick onto her back, and climbed all the way up again. The eagle family was so grateful that they took Tilly for a ride on the warm wind. She saw the whole mountain below her. When she landed, Tilly decided she didn't need wings after all. Her hooves had taken her somewhere wings could not.

## The Inventor's Mistake | an invention that went wrong
Professor Lime was trying to invent a machine that would clean her messy laboratory by itself. She built a robot with eight arms, three brushes and a vacuum nose. When she switched it on, the robot cleaned everything. It cleaned the floor, the windows and the test tubes. Then it cleaned her notes, scrubbing away all her equations. Then it tried to clean the professor's glasses right off her face. "Stop!" she shouted, but the robot kept going, polishing the cat. Professor Lime realized her mistake. She had told the robot what to do, but not when to stop. She grabbed a screwdriver, opened its panel, and added one more instruction: "When everything is clean, rest." The robot hummed, finished sweeping the last corner, and sat down quietly in its charging chair. Professor Lime laughed and wrote in her new notebook: "Every good program needs a way to stop." The cat, very shiny, did not find it funny at all.

## Two Lanterns | two lanterns at a festival
At the Loy Krathong festival, two paper lanterns sat side by side on a table, waiting to be lit. One was big and painted with golden dragons. The other was small and plain, made by a little girl named Ying from leftover paper. "I'll fly the highest," boasted the dragon lantern. "Everyone will look at me." The small lantern said nothing. When night came, people lit candles inside and let them go. The dragon lantern was so heavy with paint and decorations that it rose slowly and drifted sideways into a tree. The small lantern, light as a feather, floated straight up into the dark sky. Ying watched it climb until it became a tiny glowing star among hundreds of others above the river. She made a wish for her grandmother to get well. Later that year, her grandmother came home from the hospital, healthy again. Ying never knew if it was the lantern, but she always made her lanterns plain and light after that.
