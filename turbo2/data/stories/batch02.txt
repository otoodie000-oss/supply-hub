## The Mystery of the Missing Trophy | a school mystery
The night before the science fair, the shiny gold trophy disappeared from the school display case. Principal Wong was worried, and everyone had a theory. Detective Nok, a ten-year-old with a notebook and a magnifying glass, decided to investigate. She found three clues. First, there was glitter on the floor by the case. Second, the janitor said he heard music coming from the art room late at night. Third, the art teacher's room smelled like fresh paint. Nok followed the glitter trail to the art room and knocked. Inside, she found the art club, covered in paint, standing around the trophy. They had taken it to make a giant sculpture of it as a surprise for the science fair opening. "We were going to put it back!" said a boy with blue paint on his nose. Nok smiled and closed her notebook. The next morning, the giant golden sculpture stood in the hallway, and the real trophy sat safely in its case. Nok got a special award too: Best Detective.

## Rocket Socks | a boy who wanted to go to space
Tim wore the same pair of socks every day. They were covered in tiny rockets, and he believed they would help him become an astronaut one day. His sister said that was silly. "Astronauts need to study math and science and stay healthy," she said. Tim thought about that. He kept wearing the rocket socks, but he also started practicing math every evening. He read books about planets and learned why rockets need so much fuel to escape Earth's gravity. He ran around the park to make his heart strong. Years went by, and the socks got holes and became too small. Tim kept them in a box under his bed. One day, a letter arrived from a space agency. Tim had been chosen for astronaut training. On his first day, he pulled out the old box and tucked the tiny rocket socks into his backpack. "For luck," he whispered. His sister, now a scientist herself, laughed. "The luck," she said, "was all the hard work you did."

## The Fox and the Crow's Song | a fable about a fox and a crow
A crow sat on a branch holding a piece of cheese in her beak. A hungry fox walked by and had an idea. "Oh, beautiful crow," he said, "your feathers are so shiny. I bet your singing voice is even more beautiful. Won't you sing for me?" The crow had heard this trick before, because her grandmother had told her the old story. She carefully put the cheese under her foot and then sang a loud, croaky song. The fox's ears hurt, and he realized his plan had failed. He sat down, disappointed. The crow looked at him and felt a little sorry, because he really did look hungry. "Next time," she said, "just ask nicely." She broke off half of the cheese and dropped it down to him. The fox was so surprised that he forgot to be sneaky. "Thank you," he said. From then on, the fox and the crow were friends, and the fox learned that honesty worked much better than flattery.

## The Bus Driver's Birthday | a kind bus driver
Mr. Somsak had driven the number 9 bus for twenty years. He knew every stop, every pothole and nearly every passenger. He waved at the street sweepers and waited for the old woman who walked slowly with her cane. Nobody ever knew when his birthday was, because he never said. But one morning, a girl named Pim noticed him looking at a small card in his pocket and smiling. "Is it your birthday?" she asked. He blushed and nodded. Pim had an idea. She whispered to the other passengers, and the message spread from seat to seat. At the next stop, the baker got on with a cupcake. At the stop after that, a student handed him a drawing of the bus. By the end of the route, his dashboard was covered in notes, flowers and snacks. When the bus reached the station, all the passengers sang happy birthday. Mr. Somsak wiped his eyes and said it was the best ride of his life.

## The Dragon Librarian | a dragon who worked in a library
In the kingdom of Fernwood, the royal library was guarded by a dragon named Inkwell. Unlike most dragons, Inkwell didn't want gold. He wanted books. He had read every book in the library twice and some of them nine times. The only problem was that people were afraid to borrow books, because Inkwell was very large and accidentally sneezed small sparks when he was excited. One day, a brave boy named Felix walked in and asked for a book about the stars. Inkwell was so happy that he sneezed, and a spark landed on Felix's hat. Felix put it out with his hand and laughed. "You just need a handkerchief," he said. The next day Felix brought a giant fireproof handkerchief, and he wrote a sign for the door: "The dragon is friendly. Please ask him for recommendations." Soon the library was full of readers, and Inkwell gave the best book advice in the kingdom. He only sneezed on the really good stories.

## Midnight at the Aquarium | animals at an aquarium at night
When the last visitor left the aquarium, the sea creatures held a meeting. The octopus, Professor Eight, stood on a rock and cleared his throat. "Friends, the new baby seahorse is scared of the dark." All the creatures wanted to help. The jellyfish offered to glow. The pufferfish offered to puff up as a pillow. The sea turtles offered to sing slow lullabies. They tried each idea one by one. The glowing jellyfish were too bright. The pufferfish was too prickly. The turtles' song was so slow that they fell asleep halfway through. Finally, a small clownfish swam over and said, "When I was little, my dad stayed close to me until I fell asleep." So the whole aquarium gathered around the seahorse's tank. Nobody did anything special. They just stayed near. The little seahorse curled its tail around a sea plant, felt all its friends nearby, and drifted peacefully to sleep.

## The Champion of Tiny Things | a girl who noticed small things
Everyone in the Rangsit family was good at something big. Dad could lift heavy boxes. Mom could solve hard puzzles. Big brother could run faster than anyone at school. Little Dao thought she wasn't good at anything. But Dao noticed tiny things. She noticed when the cat was limping a little. She noticed when Grandpa was quiet because he missed Grandma. She noticed the tiny crack in the kitchen pipe before it burst. One day, the family planned a picnic at the river. Everyone was busy packing. Dao noticed dark clouds building in the west and the way the birds had suddenly gone quiet. "A storm is coming," she said. Nobody believed her, but Mom checked the forecast and found a storm warning. They had the picnic in the living room instead, with blankets and sandwiches, while thunder boomed outside. That evening, Dad gave Dao a little medal made of cardboard. It said: "Champion of Noticing." It became her favorite thing in the world.

## The Robot That Learned to Paint | a robot learning art
In a factory full of machines, there was a robot arm called Unit 7 whose job was painting cars, always the same shade of silver. One day a crate of paint fell over, and splashes of red, yellow and blue spread across the floor. Unit 7 had never seen colors mix before. Red and yellow made orange. Blue and yellow made green. It was fascinating. That night, when the factory was quiet, Unit 7 dipped its brush in the spilled paint and drew a circle on a scrap of metal. Then a sun. Then a tree. The next morning, the factory manager found the painting and stared at it for a long time. Instead of being angry, she hung it in the office. Soon workers left Unit 7 little scraps every evening. The robot painted the river, the city and the workers' smiling faces. A year later, the factory held an art show, and people came from all over town to see the paintings of the robot who discovered color.

## Saving Mr. Whiskers | rescuing a cat from a tree
Mr. Whiskers, the fattest cat on Maple Street, had climbed the tallest tree in the park and now refused to come down. Kids gathered below. "Come down, Mr. Whiskers!" they called. He meowed but didn't move. A boy named Omar tried tempting him with tuna. A girl named Sofia tried shaking a toy mouse. Nothing worked. Then little Ben, who was only five, had an idea. "Maybe he's scared because we're all so loud," he said. The kids looked at each other and sat down quietly in the grass. Ben began to hum a soft song his mom used to sing. The park became calm. After a few minutes, Mr. Whiskers stretched, looked down, and slowly began to climb, one paw at a time. When he reached the lowest branch, he hopped right into Ben's lap and started purring. The older kids cheered quietly so they wouldn't scare him again. Sometimes, Ben learned, the best way to help is to stop and be gentle.

## The Day the Colors Ran Away | colors leaving a town
One morning, the town of Brightvale woke up and everything was gray. The red roses were gray. The blue sky was gray. Even the bananas were gray. The colors had run away! A girl named Rina found a note pinned to the town fountain. It said: "Nobody ever notices us. We're going somewhere we'll be appreciated." Rina felt bad. It was true that people walked past the flowers without looking, and nobody watched sunsets anymore because they were busy with their phones. Rina gathered the town together. They wrote thank-you letters to every color. "Dear Yellow, thank you for sunflowers and egg yolks." "Dear Green, thank you for trees and frogs." They read the letters aloud in the town square. Slowly, a little pink appeared on a flower, then gold on the church bell, then the whole sky blushed orange and purple. The colors had come home. And from then on, the people of Brightvale always stopped to admire the sunset.

## The Tortoise Who Delivered Mail | a slow but reliable mail carrier
In the forest, the mail was delivered by Teo the tortoise. He was slow. Letters sometimes took weeks to arrive. The rabbits complained, and the birds laughed. So the forest hired a new mail carrier: a speedy hare named Flash. Flash delivered letters in minutes, but he was so fast that he often dropped them, mixed them up or gave them to the wrong house. The owl got the squirrel's birthday card. The badger got a love letter meant for a mole. The forest was in chaos. Meanwhile, Teo kept delivering the few letters people still gave him, slowly and carefully. Every one arrived at the right door, dry and clean. Soon, animals started bringing their important letters back to Teo. In the end, the forest decided to use both: Flash for quick notes like "dinner's ready," and Teo for anything that really mattered. Teo didn't mind sharing the job. He finally had time to stop and smell the flowers along his route.

## The Brave Little Lamp | a lamp afraid of the dark
Lolo was a small desk lamp in a boy's bedroom, and, strangely, Lolo was afraid of the dark. When the boy switched him off at night, Lolo shivered in the shadows and imagined monsters under the bed. One night, the boy had a nightmare and woke up crying. He reached over and clicked Lolo on. Lolo's warm light filled the room, and the boy saw that the monster in the corner was just his jacket on a chair. He smiled and fell back asleep with the light on. Lolo realized something important. The dark wasn't scary to him anymore, because he was the thing that made it less scary. He was made to shine for someone who needed him. From that night on, Lolo didn't mind being switched off. He knew that whenever the boy needed him, he would be ready to glow, and that being brave doesn't mean never being scared. It means helping even when you are.

## Football in the Rain | a football match in heavy rain
The final match of the school football season was supposed to be on a sunny Saturday, but the sky opened up with pouring rain. The field turned into mud. The Green Tigers and the Blue Sharks slipped and slid everywhere. Nobody could dribble properly. The ball stuck in puddles. Anan, the Tigers' goalkeeper, was covered in mud from head to toe. At half time, the score was zero to zero, and both teams were frustrated. Coach Mali gathered the Tigers. "Stop trying to play like it's sunny," she said. "Pass the ball through the air. Play the game you have, not the one you wanted." In the second half, the Tigers chipped the ball over the puddles, and with two minutes left, they scored a goal. The Sharks fought back and almost tied it, but Anan made a diving save, splashing into the biggest puddle of all. The Tigers won, but the best part was the muddy team photo afterward, where nobody could tell the teams apart.

## The Grandfather Clock's Secret | a hidden room behind a clock
When the Phan family moved into the old house, the grandfather clock in the hallway didn't tick. Eleven-year-old Mai wound it with a small key, and at exactly midnight it chimed thirteen times. Then, with a creak, the whole clock swung open like a door. Behind it was a tiny room filled with dusty toys, a wooden train set, and a letter. The letter was written seventy years ago by a boy named Henry, who had lived in the house. "To whoever finds this," it said, "please play with my train. It gets lonely." Mai and her little brother set up the tracks and ran the train around the room, its little bell ringing. Mai did some research at the library and discovered that Henry was still alive, living in a nursing home across town. The family visited him and brought the train. Henry, now ninety, laughed until he cried. "I knew someone would find it," he said, and he told them stories about the house all afternoon.

## The Hiccup Cure | a girl with endless hiccups
Pearl had the hiccups. Not normal hiccups that go away after a minute. These were champion hiccups that lasted all morning. Hic! At breakfast, she spilled her milk. Hic! In math class, she accidentally wrote the number seven upside down. Everyone had a cure. Her dad said to drink water upside down. Her friend said to hold her breath and count to twenty. Her teacher said to eat a spoon of sugar. Pearl tried everything, and nothing worked. Hic! At lunch, her little brother crept up behind her and shouted "BOO!" Pearl jumped so high that her sandwich flew into the air and landed on the principal's head. The whole cafeteria went silent. Pearl waited. No hiccup. The principal slowly took the sandwich off his head, looked at Pearl, and burst out laughing. "Well," he said, "I'm glad that worked." Pearl apologized a hundred times, but she had to admit, it was the best cure ever, even if it was a little messy.

## The Owl Who Couldn't Stay Awake | a sleepy owl
Oscar was an owl, which meant he was supposed to be awake all night and asleep all day. But Oscar had a problem: he loved the daytime. He loved watching children play in the park and seeing butterflies in the sun. As a result, he was always yawning at night, when the other owls went hunting. His parents worried. "Owls are night birds," they said. "You'll never catch mice if you keep falling asleep." One night, the forest animals got lost in a thick fog, and nobody could find the way home. The night owls couldn't see through the mist either. But Oscar knew every path, because he had watched them all in the daylight. He led the animals home one by one, calling softly through the fog. After that, the forest decided that it was good to have an owl who knew the daytime too. Oscar became the forest's daytime guide, and nobody ever complained about his yawning again.

## Lemonade for the Library | kids raising money with lemonade
The little library on Mango Street was going to close because it didn't have enough money to fix its leaky roof. Twins Kip and Noi loved that library. They decided to help. They set up a lemonade stand on the corner with a sign: "Save Our Library! 10 baht a cup." On the first day, they sold four cups, and two of those were to their mom. On the second day, it rained. Kip wanted to give up. But Noi had an idea. They asked the neighbors to donate books they no longer needed, and the twins sold the books with the lemonade. People started coming from other streets. A local newspaper wrote about them. A baker donated cookies. A musician played guitar next to the stand. By the end of the month, they had raised enough money to fix the roof. The library held a party, and on the wall by the door, they hung a small sign: "This roof was saved by lemonade."

## The Moon's Day Off | the moon taking a holiday
Every night for billions of years, the Moon had worked hard, glowing in the sky and pulling the tides. One evening, the Moon sighed. "I need a day off," she said to the stars. So she hid behind a cloud and went to sleep. Down on Earth, things got confusing. The ocean didn't know when to make the tide come in. The wolves didn't know when to howl. A little girl named Lily looked out her window and couldn't find the Moon to say goodnight. She wrote a letter: "Dear Moon, I hope you are okay. I miss you. Please come back when you are rested." She folded the letter into a paper airplane and threw it out the window. The wind carried it all the way up into the clouds. The Moon woke up, read the letter, and smiled a silver smile. She came back out that very night, a little brighter than before. Sometimes, even the Moon needs to know that someone noticed she was gone.

## The Spelling Bee Champion | a spelling contest
Daniel was terrified of the school spelling bee. Words got all tangled in his head, and when he was nervous, he spelled everything backwards. His friend Ana helped him practice every day after school with flashcards. They made silly songs for hard words. "Necessary: one collar, two sleeves," Ana sang, because necessary has one c and two s's. On the day of the contest, Daniel's knees shook. One by one, the other students made mistakes and sat down. Soon it was just Daniel and a tall girl named Grace. Grace got "rhythm" and spelled it perfectly. Daniel got "necessary." He closed his eyes and heard Ana's silly song. "N-E-C-E-S-S-A-R-Y," he said. Then Grace got "occasion" and missed a c. Daniel spelled it right and won the trophy. On stage, he held up the trophy and pointed at Ana in the audience. "She's the real champion," he said. "She taught me that hard things get easier with practice and a good song."

## The Cactus Who Wanted a Hug | a lonely cactus
In the middle of the desert lived a small cactus named Spike. All the desert animals avoided him because of his sharp needles. Spike felt lonely. He watched the lizards cuddle under rocks and the birds share their nests. "Nobody will ever hug me," he sighed. One very hot day, a little desert mouse was being chased by a hawk. She ran in circles looking for a hiding place, and the only place left was right next to Spike. She squeezed between his needles, and the hawk couldn't reach her. The hawk gave up and flew away. The mouse looked up at Spike. "Thank you for protecting me," she said. "Your needles saved my life." From then on, the mouse made her home at Spike's roots. She told the other small animals that the safest place in the desert was next to the prickly cactus. Soon Spike had more friends than anyone, and though they couldn't hug, they all felt safe together.

## The Paper Crane Wish | folding a thousand paper cranes
When Yumi's best friend Hana got sick and had to stay in the hospital for a long time, Yumi remembered an old legend: if you fold one thousand paper cranes, you can make a wish. Yumi started folding. Her fingers got tired. Her first cranes looked like crumpled frogs. But she kept going, ten cranes a day. Her classmates noticed and asked what she was doing. When she told them, they wanted to help. The whole class started folding cranes at lunch. Then the teachers joined. Then the parents. Cranes of every color filled boxes and bags. In just three weeks, they had one thousand cranes. They strung them together on long threads and brought them to the hospital. Hana's room became a rainbow of paper birds. Hana laughed for the first time in weeks. Yumi didn't know if the legend was true, but she knew that a thousand friends folding together had already made her biggest wish come true: Hana was smiling again.

## The Littlest Firefighter | a puppy at a fire station
At Station 12, the firefighters had a brand new puppy named Ember. She was small, clumsy, and constantly getting tangled in the hoses. While the big dogs at other stations were brave and calm, Ember barked at her own reflection and was scared of the siren. The firefighters loved her anyway. One afternoon, the station got a call about smoke in an apartment building. Everyone rushed out, and Ember was left behind. But she wasn't going to stay. She squeezed through the gate and followed the fire truck all the way to the building. While the firefighters worked on the flames, Ember heard a tiny meow from behind a door that nobody had checked. She barked and barked until a firefighter came to look. Inside was a frightened kitten hiding under a bed. The firefighter carried it out safely. Ember got a special little red helmet that day, and she was never scared of the siren again, because now she knew what it meant: someone needs help.

## The Bridge of Friends | two villages building a bridge
Two villages sat on opposite sides of a wide river. For years, they didn't talk to each other because of an old argument nobody even remembered. The village on the east side had lots of fruit trees but no clay for pots. The village on the west side had plenty of clay but very little fruit. One day, a girl named Lamai from the east and a boy named Kiet from the west met on two rocks in the middle of the river, both trying to catch fish. They started talking and realized how silly the argument was. They decided to build a small rope bridge between the rocks. Kids from both villages came to help, carrying ropes and planks. When the adults saw the children working together, they felt ashamed of their old fight. They joined in and built a strong wooden bridge across the whole river. Now the villages trade fruit and pots every week, and every year they hold a festival on the bridge to celebrate the day two kids decided to talk.

## The Snowflake Who Was Different | a unique snowflake
High in a winter cloud, a snowflake named Crystal was born. She looked around at all the other snowflakes and noticed that none of them looked exactly like her. Her points were a bit longer on one side, and she had a little curl in her middle. "Why am I different?" she asked an older snowflake. "Every snowflake is different," the old flake said. "No two are ever the same." Crystal didn't believe her. She worried she was strange. When they all fell together onto a hillside, Crystal landed on a little boy's mitten. The boy held her up to his magnifying glass and gasped. "Look, Dad! This one has a curl in the middle! I've never seen one like this!" He drew a careful picture of her in his notebook before she melted. That drawing hung on the boy's wall for years. Crystal never knew it, but she became the boy's favorite snowflake of all time, precisely because she was different.
