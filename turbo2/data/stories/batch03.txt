## The Mars Garden | growing plants on mars
In the year 2090, nine-year-old Zara lived in a small dome on Mars with her family. Outside, the ground was red and the sky was a dusty pink. Nothing grew there. Zara's job was to look after the greenhouse, where the family grew potatoes, beans and lettuce under special lamps. One day, Zara found a single sunflower seed in an old envelope from Earth. She planted it in the corner of the greenhouse and talked to it every morning. The adults said a sunflower needed too much light and water to grow on Mars. But Zara adjusted the lamps, measured the water carefully and kept notes like a real scientist. After weeks, a green shoot appeared. Then leaves. Then, one morning, a bright yellow flower opened, turning its face toward the lamp. People from the other domes came to see it. It was the first sunflower ever grown on Mars. Zara saved its seeds, and soon every dome on the planet had a little yellow flower to remind everyone of home.

## The Clumsy Knight | a clumsy knight
Sir Bumble was the clumsiest knight in the kingdom. He tripped over his own sword, his helmet was always on backwards, and his horse, Daisy, sighed every time he tried to climb on. When the king announced a tournament, the other knights laughed. "Bumble will fall off before the trumpet sounds!" On the day of the tournament, a wild goat escaped from the market and charged into the arena. The proud knights tried to catch it with fancy moves, but the goat dodged them all. Then Sir Bumble tripped, fell, and landed in a pile of hay, right in front of the goat. The goat was so surprised that it stopped and started eating the hay next to his head. Bumble gently put his arms around the goat's neck and gave it a scratch behind the ears. The crowd went silent, then cheered. The king gave Bumble a special medal for "Bravery in Unexpected Ways." Daisy the horse was so proud she let him climb on without a single sigh.

## The Lost Tooth Fairy | a tooth fairy who got lost
Twinkle was a brand-new tooth fairy on her very first night of work. Her map showed one house with a tooth under a pillow, but she got lost in the rain and ended up at the wrong window. Inside, an old man was sleeping, and under his pillow was a set of false teeth in a glass of water. Twinkle panicked. Was she supposed to take all of them? She sat on the windowsill and worried. A cat on the roof asked what was wrong, and Twinkle explained. The cat laughed so hard he nearly fell off. "Those aren't for you!" he said, and he showed her the right house across the street. There, a little girl named Mint had lost her first tooth. Twinkle quietly swapped it for a shiny coin. But she felt bad about the old man, so she went back and left a tiny note under his pillow: "Sorry for visiting by mistake. Have a lovely day." The next morning, the old man found the note and laughed all through breakfast.

## The Homework Machine | a machine that does homework
Eli was tired of homework, so he spent his whole weekend building a homework machine out of an old printer, a calculator and a lot of tape. You put the homework in one side, and the answers came out the other. It worked! On Monday, Eli handed in perfect math homework. The teacher was delighted. "Great job, Eli! Now come up to the board and show the class how you solved problem five." Eli froze. He had no idea how the machine solved problem five. He stood at the board for a long time, holding the chalk. Finally, he admitted the truth. The class laughed, but the teacher smiled. "Building that machine must have taken a lot of clever thinking," she said. "But the homework isn't for the answers. It's for your brain." That evening, Eli took the machine apart and turned it into a practice game that asked him math questions instead. His grades went up for real, and he never felt that panic at the board again.

## The River Race | a canoe race
Every year, the villages along the river held a big canoe race. This year, the team from Ban Nong Bua was the smallest and youngest: four kids who had only practiced for a month. The other teams had strong adults and painted boats. When the race began, the big teams paddled hard and raced ahead. But the river was tricky. There were rocks hidden under the water and a sharp bend where the current pulled toward the shore. The kids had spent their whole summer swimming in that river, so they knew exactly where the rocks were. While the big boats bumped into rocks and spun around at the bend, the kids steered smoothly along the calm side. They didn't win first place. They came in second. But the crowd cheered loudest for them, and the winning team came over to shake their hands. "You know this river better than anyone," said the captain. "Next year, will you teach us?" The kids grinned. Knowing your home, they learned, is a kind of strength too.

## The Monster Under the Bed Is Afraid | a monster afraid of a child
Gloop was a monster who lived under a bed, and he had a terrible secret: he was afraid of the little boy who slept above him. Every night, the boy, whose name was Pete, made loud snoring noises, kicked his blanket off and talked in his sleep about sharks. Gloop trembled in the dark. One night, Pete dropped his flashlight, and it rolled under the bed. He reached down and his hand touched something soft and squishy. Pete shone the light and saw a small purple monster with big frightened eyes. Pete screamed. Gloop screamed louder. Then they both stopped and stared at each other. "Are you scared of me?" Pete asked. Gloop nodded. Pete started laughing. "I've been scared of you for years!" They talked all night about scary things, sharks, thunder and the dark, and they realized everyone is afraid of something. From then on, Pete left a cookie under the bed every night, and Gloop chased away bad dreams in return.

## The Inventor Girl and the Rain | a girl who invents a rain catcher
In a dry village where water was always scarce, a girl named Amara loved to tinker. She built toys from tin cans and wind chimes from bottle caps. One year, the rains came late, and the village well nearly dried up. Amara watched the few drops that fell run off the rooftops and disappear into the dusty ground. That gave her an idea. She collected old plastic sheets, bamboo and buckets, and built a rain catcher: a big slanted sheet that guided every drop into a covered barrel. The adults were doubtful, but the next time it rained, her barrel filled to the top. Amara showed the other families how to build their own. By the end of the rainy season, every house had a rain catcher, and the village had stored enough water to last through the dry months. Amara's school invited her to give a talk. She was nervous, but she ended with a big smile and said, "Every drop matters, just like every idea."

## The Ant Who Carried a Cookie | ants working together
Andy the ant found a whole cookie crumb near the picnic blanket. It was enormous, at least a hundred times bigger than him. He tried to lift it alone. He pushed, pulled and grunted, but it barely moved. A grasshopper watched and laughed. "You'll never move that by yourself." Andy wiped his forehead and thought. Then he ran back to the anthill and came back with his friends. Ten ants lifted one side. Ten more lifted the other side. They marched in a line, carrying the crumb through the grass, around a puddle and over a stick. It took the whole afternoon, but finally they dragged it into the anthill. That night, the whole colony had a feast. The grasshopper, who had spent the afternoon napping, came by hungry. The ants shared a piece of cookie with him. "How did you do it?" he asked. Andy smiled. "Alone, I'm small," he said. "Together, we're strong enough to carry anything."

## The Snow Day Surprise | a surprise snow day
Kiri had lived in a hot country all her life, so when her family moved to a cold city in winter, she had never seen snow. Every night, she checked the weather app hoping for a snowflake symbol. One morning, she woke up and the world outside her window was completely white. Snow covered the cars, the trees and the swings in the park. School was cancelled! Kiri put on three sweaters, two pairs of socks and her dad's big gloves, and ran outside. The snow crunched under her boots. It was cold on her tongue and tasted like nothing at all. A group of neighborhood kids were building a snowman. Kiri didn't know them, and she felt shy. But a boy waved her over. "Want to help? We need someone to find the eyes!" Kiri found two smooth black pebbles. By lunchtime, they had built a snow family of five, and Kiri had five new friends. That night, she wrote in her diary: "Snow is cold, but people are warm."

## The Robot and the Puppy | a robot learning to care for a puppy
A household robot named Bolt was very good at cleaning, cooking and fixing things. One day, the family brought home a puppy named Biscuit, and Bolt was given a new task: take care of the puppy. Bolt read every manual it could find. It measured Biscuit's food to the gram and scheduled walks to the minute. But Biscuit didn't follow schedules. He chewed Bolt's cleaning brush, barked at its blinking lights and once fell asleep inside its storage compartment. Bolt was confused. None of the manuals explained this. One evening, Biscuit whined by the window during a thunderstorm. Bolt checked its manuals again, but found nothing about fear. So it did something it had never done before. It sat down on the floor next to the puppy and played soft music from its speaker. Biscuit curled up against Bolt's metal side and stopped shaking. Bolt didn't have a word for what it felt, so it wrote a new file in its memory and called it "friend."

## The Thief Who Returned Everything | a raccoon who stole shiny things
Rascal the raccoon loved shiny things. Keys, coins, spoons, bottle caps, anything that sparkled went into his secret hollow tree. The people in the neighborhood were always losing things. One day, Rascal saw a little girl crying in her yard. She had lost her grandmother's silver necklace, the only thing she had to remember her by. Rascal knew where it was. It was in his tree, right on top of his shiny pile. He felt a strange heavy feeling in his chest. That night, he carried the necklace to the girl's porch and left it on her doormat. The next morning, she found it and hugged it tightly, smiling through her tears. Rascal felt warm inside, better than any shiny thing had ever made him feel. Over the next week, keys, coins and spoons mysteriously appeared on doorsteps all over the neighborhood. People were puzzled, but happy. And Rascal decided that from now on, the shiniest thing he wanted to collect was smiles.

## The Grumpy Old Bridge | an old bridge that felt useless
Over a small stream stood an old stone bridge. For hundreds of years, it had carried horses, carts and travelers. But then a big new highway was built nearby, and nobody used the old bridge anymore. It felt forgotten and grumpy, and moss grew all over its stones. One spring, a terrible flood washed away part of the highway. Cars couldn't pass, and a family needed to get their sick grandmother to the hospital in town. They remembered the old bridge. It was narrow and bumpy, but it was still standing strong after all those years. The family drove slowly across it and made it to the hospital in time. After that, the town decided to fix up the old bridge. They cleaned its stones, planted flowers on both sides and put up a sign about its history. Now families stop there for picnics, and kids throw sticks into the stream to see whose floats fastest. The old bridge isn't grumpy anymore. It knows it still matters.

## The Midnight Baker | a baker who works at night
Every night, while the whole town slept, a baker named Mr. Toon worked in his small shop. He mixed flour, kneaded dough and slid trays of bread into the hot oven. By sunrise, the shop smelled wonderful, and people lined up for warm rolls. But Mr. Toon was lonely. He never saw the sun, and he never got to chat with anyone because he was always sleeping during the day. One night, a nurse finishing her late shift knocked on the shop window. She was hungry and tired. Mr. Toon gave her a warm roll. The next night, she came back with a bus driver who also worked nights. Then a security guard. Then a taxi driver and a street cleaner. Soon, Mr. Toon's shop had become a little midnight cafe for all the people who work while others sleep. They laughed, shared stories and drank hot tea. Mr. Toon wasn't lonely anymore. He had discovered that the night is full of friends, if you open your door.

## The Last Leaf | a leaf holding on in autumn
On a tall maple tree, as autumn turned to winter, one leaf refused to fall. All its brothers and sisters had drifted to the ground in red and gold. "Let go," the wind whispered. "It's time." But the leaf was afraid. It had spent its whole life on the branch, watching the birds and feeling the sun. It didn't know what came next. Below, a little boy looked up at it every day on his way to school. He was sick and had to walk slowly. "If that leaf can hold on," he told his mother, "so can I." The leaf heard him and held on tighter, through frost and wind and rain. One day in late winter, the boy walked by and ran. He was better. He looked up and smiled at the leaf. And the leaf, feeling that its job was done, let go at last. It floated down gently and landed right in front of the boy, who picked it up and kept it in a book, pressed flat, forever.

## The Wish Fish | a fish that grants wishes
A fisherman named Lek caught a small golden fish in his net. To his surprise, the fish spoke. "Let me go, and I will grant you three wishes." Lek thought carefully. His first wish was for his little boat to never leak again. Splash! The holes closed. His second wish was for his village to always have enough fish to eat. The fish nodded. Then Lek thought about his third wish for a long time. He could wish for gold, a palace or a giant ship. Instead he said, "My third wish is for you to be free and happy." The fish was so surprised that it leaped in the air. "In a thousand years, no one has ever used a wish for me." From then on, the golden fish followed Lek's boat, chasing away storms and guiding him to calm water. Lek never became rich, but he lived a long, happy life by the sea, with the best friend a fisherman could ever have.

## The Treehouse Club | kids building a treehouse
Four friends, Pim, Joe, Lin and Aki, decided to build a treehouse in the big mango tree behind Pim's house. They drew plans, collected old wood and borrowed tools from Pim's grandfather. The first day, they argued about everything. Joe wanted a slide. Lin wanted a rope ladder. Aki wanted a secret trap door. Pim wanted a window to see the stars. Nothing got built. Pim's grandfather watched them and laughed. "Why not build all of it?" he said. "Each of you be in charge of one part." So Joe built the slide from an old board, Lin wove a rope ladder, Aki made a tiny trap door with a hinge, and Pim cut a round window in the wall. Grandpa helped with the hard parts and made sure everything was safe. By the end of the summer, they had the best treehouse in the neighborhood. They named it the Four Corners Club, because every corner was designed by someone different, and together it was perfect.

## The Hedgehog's Balloon | a hedgehog with a balloon
Hazel the hedgehog found a red balloon tangled in a bush after a birthday party. She had always wanted a balloon, but she knew what would happen if she touched it with her spikes. Pop. She looked at it sadly. Her friend, a rabbit named Rolo, had an idea. He tied the balloon's string to a stick and gave the stick to Hazel. Now she could carry the balloon without ever touching it. Hazel walked proudly through the forest, the red balloon bobbing above her. Every animal smiled when they saw it. Then a strong wind blew, and the balloon pulled the stick right out of her paws. It floated up, up, up into the sky. Hazel was sad for a moment, but then she saw a little bird land on the balloon and ride it like a tiny airship. All the animals laughed and waved. "That's okay," Hazel said. "It was fun to have it for a while, and now someone else gets an adventure."

## The Day Dad Forgot | a dad who forgot a birthday
On the morning of Noi's tenth birthday, her dad didn't say anything. He made breakfast, read the news and rushed off to work. Noi felt her heart sink. Had he forgotten? At school, her friends sang happy birthday, but she kept thinking about her dad. She walked home slowly, kicking stones. When she opened the front door, the house was dark. Then the lights flicked on, and her whole family jumped out shouting, "Surprise!" There were balloons, a chocolate cake and a big banner that said "Happy 10th Birthday, Noi!" Her dad was wearing a silly party hat. "I'm sorry I was so quiet this morning," he said. "I'm terrible at keeping secrets, so I didn't dare say a word." Noi laughed and hugged him tightly. That night, she realized that sometimes when people seem to forget you, they are actually busy remembering you in a bigger way than you could imagine.

## The Cloud Painter | a painter of clouds
Long ago, before the sky had clouds, it was a plain, empty blue. A young painter named Aria lived on top of the tallest mountain, and she was bored of looking at the same blue every day. So she climbed onto a giant eagle's back and flew up with her brushes. She dipped her brush in fog from the valley and painted fluffy shapes across the sky: a rabbit, a castle, a ship with sails. The people below looked up and gasped. They had never seen anything like it. Children lay on the grass and named the shapes. The wind didn't like holding still, so it pushed the clouds around and changed their shapes. At first, Aria was annoyed. But then she watched a rabbit turn into a dragon, and the dragon turn into a teapot, and she laughed. Her paintings were never finished. They changed every minute. And that, she decided, was the most beautiful kind of art of all.

## The Wrong Bus | taking the wrong bus by mistake
On his first day of going to school alone, eleven-year-old Pete got on the wrong bus. He didn't notice until the bus drove past the river, which was definitely not on the way to school. His stomach dropped. He wanted to cry, but he remembered what his mom always told him: "Stay calm, think, and ask for help." Pete took a deep breath and walked up to the driver. "Excuse me, I think I'm on the wrong bus. I need to go to Riverside School." The driver smiled kindly. "Happens all the time. Get off at the next stop and take the number 4 going back. I'll call the school so they know you're coming." At the stop, an older girl from his school was waiting for the number 4 too. She showed him the way. Pete arrived at school only twenty minutes late, and his teacher wasn't angry at all. That evening, Pete told his mom the whole story, and she said she was proud of him, not for getting lost, but for how he handled it.

## The Generous Giant | a kind giant
In the hills above a small town lived a giant named Grom. Everyone was afraid of him because of his huge size and loud voice. When he sneezed, windows rattled. When he walked, the ground shook. But Grom was actually gentle and very lonely. He spent his days tending a garden of enormous vegetables: pumpkins as big as houses, carrots as long as trees. One autumn, a drought hit the town, and the crops failed. People worried about how they would eat during the winter. One morning, they woke up to find a giant pumpkin in the town square, along with a mountain of carrots and potatoes. Every week, more vegetables appeared. A curious girl named Lina followed the giant footprints back to Grom's garden. "It was you!" she said. Grom blushed so hard his ears turned red. The town held a feast in his honor, and Grom finally had friends. He still sneezed and rattled windows, but now everyone just laughed and said, "Bless you, Grom!"

## The Coding Club Robot | kids programming a robot
At Riverside School, the new coding club had one small robot and twelve excited kids. Their challenge was to program the robot to drive through a maze and pick up a ping pong ball. On the first try, the robot spun in circles. On the second try, it drove straight into a wall. The kids argued about whose code was wrong. Their teacher, Ms. Kanya, said, "Let's find the bug together, one line at a time." They went through the code step by step. They found that the robot turned left when it should have turned right, and that it waited ten seconds instead of one. They fixed each bug and tested again. Little by little, the robot got farther. On the twelfth try, it rolled through the maze, scooped up the ball and spun in a happy circle. The kids cheered so loudly the principal came to check. Ms. Kanya smiled. "Twelve tries, and you never gave up. That's what real programmers do."

## The Bravest Thing | a shy boy speaking up
Somchai was the shyest boy in his class. He never raised his hand, even when he knew the answer, and he spoke so quietly that the teacher often asked him to repeat himself. One day at lunch, he saw some older kids taking a younger boy's lunch box and laughing at him. Somchai's heart pounded. His legs felt like jelly. Everyone else just watched. Somchai walked over and said, in a voice that only shook a little, "Give it back. That's not yours." The older kids were so surprised that the quiet boy had spoken that they handed the lunch box back and walked away. The younger boy, whose name was Fai, said thank you about a hundred times. Somchai sat with him for the rest of lunch. He was still shy after that day. He still didn't raise his hand very often. But now he knew something important about himself: when it really mattered, he could find his voice. And that, his teacher said later, is what bravery really means.
