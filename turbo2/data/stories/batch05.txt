## The Bicycle That Wouldn't Stop | a runaway bicycle
When Jae got a new red bicycle, he discovered it had a mind of its own. It wouldn't go where he steered it. It zoomed down hills, rang its own bell and once rode straight into the ice cream shop. Jae was frustrated. One afternoon, the bicycle suddenly raced toward the park, no matter how hard he squeezed the brakes. It stopped right next to the pond, where a small boy was reaching for a ball floating too far out. The boy slipped. Jae jumped off the bike, grabbed the boy's hand and pulled him back onto the grass just in time. The boy's mother ran over and thanked Jae again and again. Jae looked at his bicycle, which was leaning quietly against a tree, its bell gleaming in the sun. From that day on, Jae didn't fight the bicycle quite so much. Sometimes, he thought, the strangest rides take you exactly where you're needed.

## The Lost Shoe | a shoe looking for its pair
Lefty was a blue sneaker who had lost his partner, Righty, at the swimming pool. He was found by the lost-and-found box, where he sat with a single glove, a forgotten towel and a pair of goggles. "Your owner will come back," said the glove, who had been waiting for months. Lefty wasn't so sure. Every day, he watched kids come and look through the box, but nobody took him. Then a girl named Pat came in, hopping on one foot. She was holding Righty! "I've been looking everywhere for the other one!" she said. She dug through the box, found Lefty and laughed with joy. Lefty felt like the luckiest shoe in the world. As Pat tied both laces and ran off, Lefty looked back at the glove still waiting in the box. The next day, Pat came back with a sign she had made: "Lost and Found: Please Check!" and taped it to the pool door. That week, the glove went home too.

## The Bear Who Couldn't Sleep | a bear who can't hibernate
When winter came, all the bears in the forest crawled into their dens to hibernate. But young Bruno couldn't sleep. He counted sheep, counted stars and even counted his own toes, but his eyes stayed wide open. He wandered out into the snowy forest. Everything was quiet and white. He found a family of rabbits huddled in the cold and showed them a warm hollow log. He helped a lost fox find its way home. He scared away a weasel that was sneaking up on a sleeping bird. By the time spring arrived, the forest animals had a new nickname for him: the Winter Guardian. When the other bears woke up, they heard stories about Bruno's adventures. His mother hugged him and said she was proud. "Maybe some bears are meant to sleep," she said, "and some are meant to watch over the forest while others rest." That night, at last, Bruno fell into a long and happy sleep.

## The Treasure in the Attic | finding grandpa's old letters
On a rainy Saturday, Nong and her cousin explored their grandfather's dusty attic. They hoped to find gold coins or a pirate map. Instead, they found an old wooden box filled with letters tied with string. The letters were from their grandfather to their grandmother, written when they were young and lived in different cities. He wrote about learning to cook rice without burning it, about the train that took him to work, and about how much he missed her. The girls read them all afternoon, giggling at the funny parts and getting quiet at the sweet ones. When they showed the box to their grandfather, his eyes filled with tears. He had thought the letters were lost forever. He sat with the girls and told them stories behind every letter. That evening, Nong decided that some treasures aren't made of gold. Some are made of words, saved for fifty years, waiting for someone to read them again.

## The Wind's Gift | the wind helping a windmill
A little windmill stood alone on a hill, its blades still because the wind never visited. The windmill was sad, because without turning, it couldn't help the farmer grind his grain. One day, the windmill called out, "Wind, why don't you ever come here?" A soft breeze answered. "I go everywhere, but no one ever asks me to stay." The windmill thought for a moment. "Would you stay if I danced with you?" The wind laughed and swirled around the hill. The windmill's blades creaked and began to turn, slowly at first, then faster and faster. The farmer came running, amazed, with his sacks of grain. From then on, the wind visited every day, and the windmill turned happily, grinding flour for the whole village. The baker made bread from it, and the children ate warm rolls. Nobody knew the windmill and the wind had become friends. They just knew that the hill, once so still, was now full of life.

## The Doctor's Little Helper | a girl helping at a clinic
Every Saturday, ten-year-old Fon helped at her mother's small clinic in the village. She couldn't give medicine or treat anyone, but she had other jobs. She handed out cups of water to people waiting. She played games with the children so they weren't scared. She held the hand of an old man while the nurse cleaned his cut knee. One Saturday, a little boy came in crying. He was terrified of the needle he needed for his vaccine. Fon sat next to him and told him about how vaccines work, like teaching your body a password so it can recognize germs and fight them. "It's like a training camp for your immune system," she said. The boy was so interested that he forgot to be scared. The shot was over before he noticed. "That's it?" he asked. Fon's mother smiled at her daughter. "You know," she said, "being a good helper isn't just about medicine. It's about making people feel less afraid."

## The Thunder Drum | a giant drum making thunder
In the old stories, thunder came from a giant drum kept in the clouds by a sky giant named Boomer. Every time it was about to rain, Boomer pounded his drum so the animals would know to find shelter. One day, Boomer lost his drumsticks. Rain clouds gathered, but there was no thunder. The animals below were caught in the storm, soaked and surprised. Boomer searched everywhere. Finally, a sparrow flew up to the clouds with two long reeds from the river. "Try these," she chirped. They were thin and light, and when Boomer struck the drum, it made a strange little rumble. He tried again, harder this time, and BOOM! The thunder rolled across the sky. The animals below heard it and ran for cover just before the rain poured down. Boomer thanked the sparrow and made her the official messenger of storms. That's why, the story says, you sometimes see birds flying home in a hurry just before the thunder starts.

## The Smallest Dragon | a dragon smaller than a cat
In a land of enormous dragons, there was one named Pip who was no bigger than a cat. The other dragons could fly over mountains and breathe great walls of fire. Pip could only flutter to the top of a fence and sneeze tiny sparks, enough to light a candle. One cold winter, the village below lost its fire. The wind had blown out every hearth, and the big dragons were too large to fit through the cottage doors. The villagers shivered and worried. Then Pip flew down. He squeezed through windows and chimneys, landed on every fireplace and gently breathed his tiny sparks onto the wood. One by one, the fires came back to life. Warmth and light spread through the whole village. The villagers made Pip a cozy nest by the town's biggest fireplace, and he became the most beloved dragon in the land. Sometimes, he learned, being small is exactly what makes you able to help.

## The Two Gardeners | two gardeners with different styles
Two neighbors, Mr. Lek and Mrs. Dee, both loved gardening, but they had very different styles. Mr. Lek's garden was neat and tidy, with straight rows of flowers and perfectly trimmed bushes. Mrs. Dee's garden was wild and colorful, with plants growing everywhere they pleased. They often argued over the fence about whose garden was better. One summer, a big storm knocked down the fence between them. While rebuilding it, they noticed something. Butterflies and bees flew from Mrs. Dee's wild flowers to Mr. Lek's neat rows. Birds that nested in Mrs. Dee's messy bushes ate the bugs that bothered Mr. Lek's plants. Each garden was helping the other. They decided not to rebuild the fence at all. Instead, they planted a path of flowers right through the middle, half neat and half wild. Neighbors came from all over to walk through it. Mr. Lek and Mrs. Dee still argued, but now they did it over tea in the middle of the garden.

## The Map Maker | a girl who makes maps
Eleven-year-old Ana loved maps. She drew maps of her house, her school and her neighborhood, marking every tree and puddle. One day, the town's old paper map at the bus stop was destroyed by rain. A new family who had just moved in got lost trying to find the hospital. Ana had an idea. She took her colored pencils and walked through every street, drawing carefully. She marked the hospital, the library, the market, the school and the park. She even marked the best place to see the sunset. When she finished, she showed it to the town mayor. The mayor was so impressed that the town printed Ana's map and put copies at every bus stop and in the library. People started using it every day. Ana added her signature in the bottom corner, tiny but proud. Years later, she became a real mapmaker who made maps of faraway places. But she always kept a copy of her very first map in her desk.

## The Ice Cream Truck's Last Day | an ice cream man retiring
For thirty years, Mr. Chai drove his ice cream truck through the neighborhood, playing the same cheerful tune. Every child knew the song by heart. When they heard it, they ran to their parents for coins. One summer, Mr. Chai announced that he was retiring. His back was tired, and his truck was getting old. On his last day, the children of the neighborhood planned a surprise. When Mr. Chai drove down the last street, he found the whole neighborhood waiting, kids and grown-ups who had been kids when he started. They held a giant sign that said "Thank You, Mr. Chai!" Everyone had a story: the time he gave a free cone to a crying boy, the time he waited an extra ten minutes for a girl who had to finish her homework. Mr. Chai gave out free ice cream to everyone until his freezer was empty. Then they all sang his song together, and he drove away waving, his eyes shining.

## The Upside Kite Contest | a kite contest with homemade kites
The village held a kite contest every year during the windy season. Most kids bought shiny kites from the market, but nine-year-old Tee had no money. He made a kite from old newspaper, bamboo sticks and a tail of colorful rags. The other kids laughed at it. On contest day, the wind was strong and wild. The shiny plastic kites flapped and spun out of control. Some got tangled together. Tee's newspaper kite was light and balanced, and it rose steadily into the sky, its rag tail dancing. It climbed higher and higher until it was a tiny speck among the clouds. The judges announced the winner: Tee, for the highest-flying kite. The other kids gathered around and asked how he made it. Tee happily showed them. The next year, the whole village held a new contest: the Homemade Kite Challenge, where every kite had to be built from things people already had at home.

## The Robot's First Joke | a robot learning humor
Unit B-12 was a helpful robot who could answer any question, but it didn't understand jokes. When people laughed, B-12 analyzed the sounds and wondered why. A boy named Leo decided to teach it. "A joke is when you expect one thing, but something surprising happens," he said. "Why did the scarecrow win an award? Because he was outstanding in his field!" B-12 processed this. Outstanding meant excellent, but it also meant standing out in a field. The words had two meanings at once. B-12's lights blinked. It tried to make its own joke. "Why did the robot go to school? To improve its… input." Leo laughed so hard he fell off his chair. It wasn't the funniest joke, but it was B-12's first. B-12 recorded the sound of Leo's laughter and decided it was a very good sound. From then on, B-12 practiced a new joke every day. Most of them were terrible, which, Leo said, made them even funnier.

## The Firefly Festival | fireflies lighting a festival
In a small village by the rice fields, the annual festival was in trouble. A storm had knocked out the electricity, and there were no lights for the evening celebration. The children were disappointed. The festival was always the most magical night of the year. An old woman named Grandma Bua told them to wait until dark and walk to the edge of the fields. When the sun set, thousands of fireflies rose from the grass, blinking gold and green. They swirled around the trees like floating lanterns. The villagers brought out drums and flutes and started the festival right there among the fireflies. They danced, sang and told stories under the glowing sky. It was the most beautiful festival anyone could remember. From that year on, the village held the festival in the fields every year, with no electric lights at all. They called it the Firefly Festival, and they took great care to keep the fields clean so the fireflies would always come back.

## The Shy Star Student | a quiet student good at math
Lena was the best math student in her class, but nobody knew it, because she never spoke up. When the teacher asked questions, Lena knew the answers, but she whispered them to herself. One day, the school announced a math competition between classes. Lena's class needed a team of three. Nobody volunteered for the last spot. Lena's hand trembled, but she slowly raised it. Her classmates were surprised. During the competition, the problems got harder and harder. Her two teammates got stuck on the final question. Lena took a deep breath, picked up the marker and solved it on the whiteboard, explaining every step. Her class won! Her classmates cheered and lifted her onto their shoulders. Afterward, a girl from her class asked if Lena could help her study. Lena said yes. By the end of the year, she had started a small math club that met every Friday. She was still quiet, but now she knew her voice was worth sharing.

## The Gecko Who Sang at Night | a gecko singing
In a house in Bangkok lived a gecko named Took. Every night, he climbed the wall next to the window and sang his song: "Tukk-kae! Tukk-kae!" The family's little girl, May, loved counting how many times he sang. Her grandmother said that if a gecko sang seven times in a row, it meant good luck. May listened every night, but Took always stopped at four, five or six. One evening, May was nervous because she had a big test the next day. She lay in bed listening. "Tukk-kae! Tukk-kae! Tukk-kae! Tukk-kae! Tukk-kae! Tukk-kae!" Six. May held her breath. Then, after a long pause, one more: "Tukk-kae!" Seven! May fell asleep smiling. The next day, she did well on her test. Was it the gecko? Probably not, said her grandmother. It was because she studied hard and got a good night's sleep. But May still left a thank-you note on the windowsill, just in case.

## The Grown-Up Who Forgot How to Play | an adult rediscovering play
Mr. Park was a very serious grown-up. He wore a gray suit, carried a gray briefcase and never had time to play. One day, his car broke down near a park, and he had to wait an hour for help. He sat stiffly on a bench. A little girl was building a sandcastle nearby. "Do you want to help?" she asked. "I don't play," said Mr. Park. "Everybody plays," said the girl, and she handed him a plastic shovel. Mr. Park sighed and dug a little moat. Then he added a bridge from a stick. Then a tower made from a paper cup. Before he knew it, he had taken off his jacket and was laughing as he decorated the castle with seashells. When the tow truck arrived, Mr. Park was covered in sand and smiling. The next weekend, he came back to the park with his own bucket and shovel. The little girl was there again. "I thought you didn't play," she said. "I forgot that I did," he replied.

## The Garden on the Roof | a rooftop garden in the city
In a busy city full of concrete and cars, eleven-year-old Mali lived in an apartment with no garden. She missed the green fields at her grandmother's house in the countryside. One day, she climbed the stairs to the building's flat roof. It was empty except for some old pipes and puddles. Mali had an idea. With her parents' permission, she collected old buckets, broken pots and wooden boxes. She filled them with soil and planted herbs, tomatoes and flowers. At first, the neighbors thought she was silly. But soon, an old man from the third floor brought her some chili seedlings. A young couple brought a small lemon tree. A family from the top floor built a bench. Within a year, the roof had become a green garden with vegetables, flowers and buzzing bees. Neighbors who had never spoken before met there every evening to water plants and watch the sunset over the city. Mali's grandmother visited and said it was the most beautiful garden she had ever seen.

## The Brave Duckling | a duckling learning to swim
All the ducklings in the pond were learning to swim, except for the smallest one, Dot. She was afraid of the water. When her brothers and sisters paddled happily around the pond, Dot stayed on the bank, watching. "What if I sink?" she worried. Her mother didn't push her. Each day, she let Dot dip just one foot in. Then two feet. Then she sat in the shallow water by the reeds, where it only reached her belly. One afternoon, a gust of wind blew a leaf onto the water, and on the leaf was a tiny beetle, struggling not to fall off. Dot didn't think. She paddled straight out, nudged the leaf back to shore and saved the beetle. Then she realized where she was. She was swimming, in the middle of the pond! Her family quacked and cheered. Dot learned that sometimes the best way to stop being afraid is to focus on someone else who needs help.

## The Detective Dog | a dog solving a mystery
Sherlock was a beagle with a nose for mysteries. When the neighbor's prize-winning pie disappeared from her windowsill, Sherlock was on the case. He sniffed the windowsill: apple and cinnamon. He sniffed the garden: apple, cinnamon and the smell of a cat. He followed the trail past the mailbox, under a fence and around a tree. Finally, he found the culprit: not the cat, but a family of squirrels feasting on the pie behind the shed. The cat had only passed by. Sherlock barked proudly until the neighbor came out. She laughed when she saw the squirrels, their faces covered in crumbs. "Well, I can't arrest squirrels," she said. "But you, Sherlock, deserve a reward." She baked a small dog-friendly treat just for him. From then on, the neighborhood called Sherlock whenever something went missing: lost keys, a runaway hamster and once even a missing grandpa, who had fallen asleep in a hammock two gardens over.

## The Night Market Adventure | visiting a night market
On Friday evening, Pim's family went to the night market for the first time. Colorful lights hung over rows of stalls. The air smelled of grilled chicken, sweet coconut and spicy papaya salad. Pim's eyes went wide. There were stalls selling toys, clothes, fresh fruit and tiny goldfish in plastic bags. A man made pancakes shaped like animals. A woman sold mango sticky rice so delicious that Pim ate two servings. Her dad bought her a little wooden flute from an old craftsman, who showed her how to play a simple tune. Pim's little brother won a stuffed elephant at a ring-toss game. As they walked home, full and tired, Pim played her new flute softly. "Can we come back next week?" she asked. Her mom laughed. "If you finish your homework early." Pim had never finished her homework so fast as she did the next Friday, just so she could hear the night market music again.

## The Wishing Well That Listened | a well that grants listening instead of wishes
In the middle of a village stood an old well, and people said it granted wishes. They threw coins in and whispered their hopes: a new bicycle, good grades, rain for their crops. But the wishes never came true, and people stopped visiting. Only a lonely old woman named Grandma Sai kept coming. She didn't make wishes. She just talked to the well about her day, her garden and the grandchildren who lived far away. One day, a boy overheard her. He felt sorry for her and started visiting too. He told the well his worries about school. Then his friends came, and their parents. Soon, the well was the busiest place in the village, not because it granted wishes, but because people met there and talked. They helped each other with the things they had wished for. They fixed bicycles together, studied together and shared water from their wells during dry seasons. Grandma Sai smiled. "The well always granted wishes," she said. "It just needed us to listen."

## The Brave Little Boat | a tugboat in a storm
In a busy harbor, the big ships laughed at Tug, the smallest tugboat. "You're too tiny to matter," they said, as they sailed off on long voyages. Tug's job was to push and pull the big ships safely into the harbor, but nobody noticed him. One night, a terrible storm hit. A huge cargo ship lost its engine and drifted toward the rocks at the harbor entrance. Waves crashed over the docks. The other boats were too big to move quickly in the narrow harbor. But Tug was small and strong. He chugged out through the waves, his little engine roaring. He threw a rope to the cargo ship, pulled with all his might and slowly turned it away from the rocks. By morning, the cargo ship was safe at the dock. The captain thanked Tug in front of the whole harbor. After that night, the big ships never laughed at Tug again. They blew their horns in greeting every time he passed.

## The Grumpy Cloud and the Rainbow | a cloud that learns to smile
Gruff was a thundercloud who loved being grumpy. He rumbled at the birds and dumped rain on picnics. "Nobody likes rain," he grumbled. "So I might as well be mean." One afternoon, after a big storm, Gruff noticed the sun peeking out behind him. Its light shone through his last raindrops, and suddenly, a rainbow stretched across the whole sky. Down below, children pointed and cheered. A farmer smiled at his wet, happy fields. A little girl ran outside with her paint set to paint the colors. Gruff was confused. "They're happy because of me?" he asked the sun. "Your rain and my light make something beautiful together," said the sun. "And your rain makes the flowers grow." Gruff looked down at the green fields and the blooming flowers and felt something new. It wasn't grumpy. It was proud. From then on, Gruff still rained when he needed to, but he always made sure to leave room for the sun.
